# Pickled young honey fungus: why there is no recipe here

There is no recipe here for young honey fungus (opieńki) in a vinegar pickling liquid. [T:-]

## !

- Drying and cooking do not destroy the toxins of poisonous mushrooms, and only an expert can tell edible species from poisonous ones. [T:SUS-035]

## Why

NCHFP warns: do not can wild mushrooms; its tested canning process is only for small to medium-size cultivated mushrooms with unopened caps. [T:ACI-030]

JarAtlas treats mushrooms pickled in vinegar and processed in jars in a boiling-water bath as canned mushrooms, so it applies this warning to them too. [T:-]

The FDA list gives mushrooms a pH of about 6.00–6.70, while acid foods are foods with a natural pH of 4.6 or lower. [T:ACI-159,ACI-001]

According to the Polish State Sanitary Inspection, honey fungus is an edible mushroom, but only young caps are fit to eat. [T:SUS-335]

According to ATS Milano, a public health agency, honey fungus (Armillaria mellea) is toxic raw or undercooked; to make it edible it must first be boiled for at least 20 minutes without a lid and the water thrown away, because it contains toxins that are inactivated by heat. [T:SUS-336]

JarAtlas has found no tested recipe for wild mushrooms in vinegar from a checked source. [T:-]
