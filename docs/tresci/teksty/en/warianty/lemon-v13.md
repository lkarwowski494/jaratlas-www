# Lemon salt (UC Master Food Preservers)

This card describes citrus salt made from lemon zest and salt, dried in the air, in a dehydrator or in the oven, following two UC Master Food Preserver handouts. [T:SUS-329,SUS-330]

## Ingredients and preparing

According to the Sacramento County UCCE Master Food Preservers, use 1 cup of flake or coarse salt and 3 tablespoons of citrus zest; a US cup holds about 237 ml. [T:SUS-329,ACI-195]

Zest only the coloured outer peel and avoid the white pith. [T:SUS-330]

Work the zest into the salt with your fingers, then spread the mixture on a baking tray. [T:SUS-329]

## Drying

Air-dry the mixture until it is completely dry, which takes 8 hours to overnight (UCCE Sacramento). [T:SUS-329]

According to the UC ANR handout, the salt can also be dried in a dehydrator at 125 °F (about 52 °C) or in a 220 °F (105 °C) oven. [T:SUS-330]

The salt is done when the zest feels dry and crumbles when pinched. [T:SUS-330]

## Packing and storing

Pack only the completely cooled salt: warm dried food sweats, and that moisture can be enough for mould to grow. [T:SUS-024]

Store the salt in clean, labelled jars; an optional desiccant pack helps keep it dry. [T:SUS-330]

Packaging must be food grade: do not use trash bags or plastic or fibreboard containers that previously held non-food items. [T:SUS-065]

Keep the jar airtight at room temperature for up to 2 months (UCCE Sacramento). [T:SUS-329]

Store the jar in a cool, dry, dark place. [T:SUS-066]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): the UC ANR handout gives the salt its best flavour in the first 3 months, and this card gives the shorter limit of 2 months. [T:SUS-329,SUS-330]

## When to throw away

Check the salt often (glass shows condensation); if it has taken up moisture but is not spoiled, use it at once or dry it again and repack it. [T:SUS-023]

Throw away mouldy salt; also throw away salt that smells off. [T:SUS-022]
