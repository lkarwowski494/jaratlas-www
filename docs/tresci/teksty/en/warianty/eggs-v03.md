# Pickled eggs: the tested NCHFP dilled eggs, no curry version

NCHFP has no recipe for pickled eggs in a curry or turmeric brine, so this card describes the NCHFP recipe for dilled eggs, which are kept only in the refrigerator. [T:MIE-135,MIE-082]

## !

- Home pickled eggs stored at room temperature have caused botulism, according to a CDC report. [T:MIE-083]
- Never keep pickled eggs at room temperature except when serving, and then for no more than 2 hours in the 40–140 °F (about 4–60 °C) zone. [T:MIE-084,ACI-034]
- It is not safe to can pickled eggs at home; all NCHFP pickled egg recipes are for the refrigerator. [T:MIE-090,MIE-082]

## What this card covers

The NCHFP pickled egg recipes are red beet, sweet and sour, dark and spicy, cidered, dilled and pineapple eggs; none of them uses curry or turmeric. [T:MIE-135]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): JarAtlas has no tested source for adding curry, turmeric or other spices outside the recipe, so this card does not give them; do not change the amounts of vinegar, water or salt. [T:MIE-135]

Eggs are a low-acid food (pH 6.4–9.0), and vinegar may not penetrate every part of the egg. [T:MIE-087]

## Ingredients

Each NCHFP recipe uses 12 peeled, hard-cooked eggs. [T:MIE-131]

Eggs for pickling should have clean, sound shells; small or medium eggs are usually a good choice, so that the seasoning can penetrate. [T:MIE-133]

The dilled eggs pickling solution is made with 1 1/2 cups of white vinegar, 1 cup of water and 3 teaspoons of salt. [T:MIE-132]

The seasonings are 3/4 teaspoon of dill weed, 1/4 teaspoon of white pepper, 1/4 teaspoon of mustard seed, 1/2 teaspoon of onion juice or minced onion, and 1/2 teaspoon of minced garlic or 1 peeled garlic clove. [T:MIE-132]

## Cooking the eggs

Make a pinhole in the large end of each egg, place the eggs in a single layer in a saucepan and cover them with cold water to an inch above the eggs. [T:MIE-134]

Put a lid on the pan, bring the water to a boil, turn down the heat and simmer for 15 minutes. [T:MIE-134]

Place the eggs in cold water and peel them when cool, starting at the large end; you can peel them under cold running water. [T:MIE-134]

## Pickling

Bring all the ingredients except the eggs to a boil, reduce the heat and simmer for 5 minutes. [T:MIE-131,MIE-088]

Pack no more than one dozen eggs loosely into a warm, pre-sterilized quart jar, or a similar container that can be closed tightly. [T:MIE-131]

There must be plenty of pickling solution, enough to cover the eggs completely. [T:MIE-131]

Pour the hot solution over the eggs, cover the jar and refrigerate it immediately. [T:MIE-131]

## Storing

Keep the eggs refrigerated at all times. [T:MIE-133]

The eggs must stay completely covered with the pickling solution throughout storage. [T:MIE-089]

Small eggs usually need 1–2 weeks to take up the flavor, and medium or large eggs may need 2–4 weeks. [T:MIE-133]

Use the eggs within 3–4 months for best quality. [T:MIE-085]
