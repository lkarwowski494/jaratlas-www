# Pork shoulder chunks in their own juices (NCHFP, pressure canner)

This card describes the NCHFP recipe for strips, cubes or chunks of meat in a pressure canner, here made with pork shoulder or neck. [T:SZY-078]

## Pressure canner only

Pressure canning is the only recommended method for canning meat, poultry, seafood and vegetables; using a boiling-water bath for these foods poses a real risk of botulism poisoning. [T:SZY-006]

Low-acid foods have a pH above 4.6; they include red meats, seafood, poultry, milk and all fresh vegetables except most tomatoes. [T:SZY-001]

All low-acid foods should be sterilized at 240 to 250 °F (about 116 to 121 °C), temperatures reached in a pressure canner operated at 10 to 15 PSIG. [T:SZY-003]

Botulinum spores are very hard to destroy at boiling-water temperatures; the higher the canner temperature, the more easily they are destroyed. [T:SZY-004]

A boiling-water bath is suitable only for acid foods, such as fruits, pickles, jams and fruit butters, and only with the research-based process times of the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning. [T:SZY-054]

Do not add butter, ghee or other ingredients to jars in tested canning processes unless the original instructions call for them. [T:SZY-013]

## The canner

NCHFP does not recommend USDA canning processes in electric pressure multi-cookers, even if the manufacturer’s directions include canning; its pressure processes were developed for stovetop pressure canners that hold four or more quart jars standing upright. [T:SZY-062]

USDA recommends that a canner hold at least 4 quart jars to be considered a pressure canner for its published processes. [T:SZY-056]

A pressure canner has either a dial gauge, which shows the pressure, or a weighted gauge, which shows and regulates it; on a dial-gauge canner read the pressure only from the dial. [T:SZY-056]

A weighted gauge usually jiggles several times a minute or keeps rocking gently while it holds the correct pressure; the manufacturer’s directions say exactly how. [T:SZY-056]

Check a dial gauge for accuracy every year before use; if it reads high or low by more than 2 lb at 5, 10 or 15 lb pressure, replace it. [T:SZY-061]

If it is off by less than 2 lb, NCHFP says adjustments can be made to reach the required pressure; this card does not describe them. [T:SZY-061]

An inaccurate dial gauge is one cause of serious errors in pressure canning. [T:SZY-061]

## Preparing the meat

NCHFP gives this recipe for strips, cubes or chunks of bear, beef, lamb, pork, veal or venison. [T:SZY-078]

Choose high-quality chilled meat, remove excess fat and remove large bones. [T:SZY-078]

Cut the pork into strips, cubes or chunks. [T:SZY-078]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): do not thicken the liquid or add flour or other ingredients the recipe does not call for. [T:SZY-013]

## Hot pack or raw pack

NCHFP prefers the hot pack for the best liquid cover and quality in storage, because the natural fat and juices of today’s leaner cuts are usually not enough to cover the meat in a raw pack. [T:SZY-078]

Hot pack: precook the meat until rare by roasting, stewing or browning it in a small amount of fat. [T:SZY-078]

If you like, add 1 teaspoon of salt per quart jar (1/2 teaspoon per pint jar). [T:SZY-078]

Fill the jars with the pieces and add boiling broth, meat drippings, water or tomato juice, leaving 1 inch (about 2.5 cm) of headspace. [T:SZY-078]

Raw pack: add salt if you like as above, fill the jars with raw meat pieces, leaving 1 inch of headspace, and do not add liquid. [T:SZY-078]

Put on the lids. [T:-]

## Own juices

For pork in its own juices, use the hot pack and cover the pieces with boiling meat drippings or broth, two of the liquids the recipe allows. [T:SZY-078]

## Loading, venting and timing

Before preparing the food, make sure the canner works properly: clean the gasket and other parts as the manufacturer directs, and check that the vent pipes are clear, with no trapped material or mineral deposits. [T:SZY-055]

Put the rack and hot water in the canner; if the recipe gives no amount, use enough water to be 2 to 3 inches (about 5 to 7.6 cm) deep. [T:SZY-055]

For hot-packed jars you can heat the water to 180 °F (about 82 °C) beforehand, without letting it boil; for raw-packed jars heat it only to 140 °F (60 °C). [T:SZY-055]

Place the filled jars, with lids and screw bands on, on the rack with a jar lifter, keeping them upright at all times. [T:SZY-055]

Fasten the canner lid, leaving the weight off the vent pipe or the petcock open. [T:SZY-055]

Air trapped in the canner lowers the temperature at a given pressure and leads to underprocessing, so vent the canner for 10 minutes before pressurizing it. [T:SZY-057]

Heat on high until the water boils and steam flows freely in a funnel shape from the open vent pipe or petcock; then, keeping the heat on high, let the steam flow continuously for 10 minutes. [T:SZY-057]

After venting, put the counterweight or weighted gauge on the vent pipe or close the petcock; the canner pressurizes over the next 3 to 10 minutes. [T:SZY-057]

Start timing when the dial gauge shows the required pressure or, on a canner without a dial gauge, when the weighted gauge starts to jiggle or rock as the manufacturer describes. [T:SZY-058]

Adjust the heat to keep a steady pressure at or slightly above the required pressure; quick, large changes in pressure can make the jars lose liquid. [T:SZY-058]

If at any time the pressure drops below the required pressure, bring the canner back up to pressure and time the process again from the beginning, using the full original time; a loss of pressure can leave the food underprocessed and unsafe. [T:SZY-058]

## Pressure and time

Process hot- or raw-packed pints for 75 minutes and quarts for 90 minutes. [T:SZY-078]

Inside the canner the pressure, and so the temperature, is lower at higher elevations, so the canner must be operated at a higher pressure as elevation increases. [T:SZY-063]

Dial-gauge canner: 11 lb at 0–2000 ft (up to 600 m), 12 lb at 2001–4000 ft (above 600 m up to 1200 m), 13 lb at 4001–6000 ft (above 1200 m up to 1800 m) and 14 lb at 6001–8000 ft (above 1800 m up to 2400 m). [T:SZY-078,SZY-064]

Above 8000 ft (above 2400 m) the dial-gauge table gives no pressure, so this card gives no dial-gauge process there. [T:SZY-065,SZY-064]

Weighted-gauge canner: 10 lb at 1000 ft or less (300 m or less) and 15 lb above 1000 ft (above 300 m). [T:SZY-078,SZY-064]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 2000 ft 600 m, 4000 ft 1200 m, 6000 ft 1800 m and 8000 ft 2400 m. [T:SZY-064]

Pressures are in pounds per square inch (lb, PSI), as in the NCHFP tables. [T:-]

NCHFP times are for US jar sizes: a pint holds about 473 ml and a quart about 946 ml. [T:ACI-174]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): for a jar of up to 473 ml use the pint time, and for a larger jar of up to 946 ml, for example 0.5 l or 0.9 l, use the quart time. [T:ACI-175]

This recipe gives no time for jars larger than a quart, such as 1 l, so JarAtlas recommends smaller jars. [T:ACI-175]

## After processing

When the time is up, turn off the heat, take the canner off an electric burner if you can (lift it, do not slide it; it may also stay where it is) and let it cool down naturally; as it cools, it loses pressure. [T:SZY-059]

Do not force-cool the canner: cooling it with cold running water or opening the vent pipe before it is fully depressurized can spoil the food, make the jars lose liquid and cause seal failures. [T:SZY-059]

Even when the dial reads zero, remove the weight cautiously: tilt it slightly first to make sure no steam escapes. [T:SZY-059]

Do not force the lid open if the cover lock has not released. [T:SZY-059]

When the canner is completely depressurized, remove the weight or open the petcock, wait 10 minutes, then unfasten the lid and lift it with the underside away from you so the steam does not burn your face. [T:SZY-060]

Lift the jars out one at a time with a jar lifter without tilting them, and set them on a towel or cooling rack at least 1 inch (about 2.5 cm) apart, not on a cold surface or in a cold draft. [T:SZY-060]

Do not tighten the bands or press the centre of the flat lid until the jar has cooled completely. [T:SZY-060]

When you take the hot jars out of the canner, do not retighten the lids or bands; this can cut through the gasket and cause seal failures. [T:SLO-210]

In raw-packed jars the food and liquid level will be noticeably lower after cooling. [T:SLO-210]

If any jar loses a lot of liquid during processing, do not open it to add more. [T:SLO-210]

## Cooling and storing

Cool the jars at room temperature for 12 to 24 hours, then remove only the screw bands (the metal rings of two-piece lids), leaving the flat lids on the jars, and test the seal: press the middle of the lid, and if it springs up when you release it, the jar is not sealed. [T:SLO-196,SLO-212]

NCHFP calls Mason-type canning jars with self-sealing two-piece lids (a flat lid and a screw band) the best choice, and this seal test and the method on this card assume them; NCHFP does not recommend other commercial jars whose mouths cannot be sealed with two-piece canning lids for canning any food at home. [T:SLO-223,SLO-212]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): use this card only with jars that take two-piece canning lids; it gives no method for twist-off jars or jars with glass lids, so do not use them for this recipe. [T:SLO-223]

A sealed lid is concave, curved down slightly in the centre; if the centre is flat or bulging, the jar may not be sealed. [T:SLO-196]

If a jar has not sealed at this check, keep it in the refrigerator and eat it within several days. [T:SLO-197]

Store sealed jars in a clean, cool, dark, dry place, not above 95 °F (35 °C) and not near hot pipes, a stove or a furnace, in an uninsulated attic or in direct sunlight; in such conditions the food may spoil. [T:SLO-198]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): keep an opened jar in the refrigerator and use it within 1 week. [T:SLO-322]

## When to throw it out

Before using a stored jar, check that the lid is tight and holds a vacuum: a good seal has a concave centre. [T:SLO-191]

Look for streaks of dried food running from the top of the jar, rising air bubbles and unnatural colour. [T:SLO-191]

When opening the jar, check for an unnatural smell, spurting liquid and cotton-like mould on the food or under the lid. [T:SLO-191]

Do not taste food from a jar with an unsealed lid or with signs of spoilage. [T:SLO-190]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): throw out such a jar. [T:SLO-190]

NCHFP warns that spoiled low-acid foods, including tomatoes, may show different signs of spoilage or very few, so every suspect jar of such food should be treated as if it had produced botulinum toxin and handled carefully. [T:SLO-350]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): handle every suspect jar from this card this way, because its main ingredient is low in acid or close to the acidity limit. [T:SLO-350]

If a suspect jar is still sealed, put it in a heavy garbage bag, close the bag and put it in a regular trash container. [T:SLO-350]

If it is unsealed, open or leaking, NCHFP says it should be detoxified before disposal; JarAtlas does not repeat that procedure here, so follow the detoxification and clean-up steps on the NCHFP page “Identifying and Handling Spoiled Canned Food” (nchfp.uga.edu/how/can/general-information/identifying-and-handling-spoiled-canned-food/) and avoid contact with suspect food or liquid. [T:SLO-350]

Contact with botulinum toxin can be fatal if it is swallowed or enters through any openings in the skin, so wear rubber or heavy plastic gloves when handling suspect jars and cleaning up. [T:SLO-350]
