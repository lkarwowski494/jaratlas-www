# Spiced pear pickles with cloves and cinnamon (NCHFP)

This card describes the NCHFP recipe for pear pickles: peeled pickling pears simmered in a vinegar and sugar syrup with cinnamon, cloves and allspice, hot packed and processed in a boiling-water bath. [T:SLO-634,SLO-637,SLO-638]

## Which pears

The recipe uses Seckel pears or other pickling pears; halve or quarter large pears. [T:SLO-634,SLO-636]

NCHFP calls Asian pears borderline in acidity and gives them a separate acidifying procedure; this recipe does not name them, so this card does not cover Asian pears (nashi). [T:ACI-064]

Besides cloves and cinnamon, the recipe also contains allspice. [T:SLO-634]

## Why you must not change the recipe

The whole product must contain a minimum, uniform level of acid so that botulism bacteria cannot grow. [T:ACI-017]

Do not change the proportions of vinegar, fruit and water and do not use vinegar of unknown acidity; use only recipes with tested proportions. [T:ACI-016]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): do not change the amounts of vinegar, water or pears and do not add other vegetables; the recipe is tested only in the proportions given. [T:ACI-265]

White distilled vinegar and cider vinegar of 5% acidity are recommended for pickles. [T:ACI-018]

Penn State Extension advises vinegar of 5–7% acidity for home food preservation and advises against diluting vinegar with water unless a research-tested recipe says so. [T:ACI-209,ACI-210]

JarAtlas rule (cautious, for readers outside the USA): use the white distilled (spirit) vinegar the recipe lists, labelled 5% or 6% acidity; do not use 10% vinegar and do not dilute it with water to make 5% vinegar; add only the water the recipe lists. [T:ACI-211,ACI-018]

## Ingredients

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

For about 7 or 8 pint jars NCHFP lists 8 cups (2 quarts) of sugar, 4 cups (1 quart) of white vinegar (5%), 2 cups (1 pint) of water and 8 pounds (about 3.6 kg; 4 to 5 quarts) of Seckel or other pickling pears. [T:SLO-634]

Spices: 8 cinnamon sticks in pieces 2 inches (about 5.1 cm) long, 2 tablespoons of whole cloves and 2 tablespoons of whole allspice. [T:SLO-634]

## Making the syrup

Combine the sugar, vinegar, water and cinnamon sticks, and add the cloves and allspice tied in a clean, thin, white cloth. [T:SLO-635]

Bring to a boil and simmer, covered, for about 30 minutes. [T:SLO-635]

## Preparing the pears

Wash the pears, remove the skins and all of the blossom end; you may leave the stems on. [T:SLO-636]

Halve or quarter large pears. [T:SLO-636]

To keep peeled pears from darkening, put them right after peeling into a cold solution of 1/2 teaspoon of ascorbic acid per 2 quarts (about 1.9 l) of water. [T:SLO-636]

Drain them just before using. [T:SLO-636]

## Cooking and filling

Add the drained pears to the hot syrup, bring to a boil, then reduce the heat and simmer for another 20 to 25 minutes. [T:SLO-637]

Pack the hot pears into hot pint jars and add to each jar one piece of cinnamon stick 2 inches (about 5.1 cm) long. [T:SLO-637]

Cover the pears with boiling syrup, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace; the syrup must cover the pears. [T:SLO-637]

Remove air bubbles and adjust the headspace if needed. [T:SLO-637]

Wipe the rims with a damp clean paper towel, put on two-piece metal canning lids and process. [T:SLO-637]

## Processing

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process pint jars in a boiling-water bath for 20 minutes. [T:SLO-638,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–3000 ft (above 300 m up to 900 m) process them for 25 minutes. [T:SLO-639,ACI-176]

At 3001–6000 ft (above 900 m up to 1800 m) process them for 30 minutes. [T:SLO-640,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 35 minutes. [T:SLO-641,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 3000 ft becomes 900 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

The NCHFP table gives times only for pint jars. [T:SLO-638]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): a pint holds about 473 ml, so a 0.5 l jar has no tested time in this recipe; use jars of up to 473 ml. [T:ACI-179,ACI-174]

## Cooling and storing

When you take the hot jars out of the canner, do not retighten the lids or bands; this can cut through the gasket and cause seal failures. [T:SLO-210]

Cool the jars at room temperature for 12 to 24 hours, then remove only the screw bands (the metal rings of two-piece lids), leaving the flat lids on the jars, and test the seal: press the middle of the lid, and if it springs up when you release it, the jar is not sealed. [T:SLO-196,SLO-212]

NCHFP calls Mason-type canning jars with self-sealing two-piece lids (a flat lid and a screw band) the best choice, and this seal test and the method on this card assume them; NCHFP does not recommend other commercial jars whose mouths cannot be sealed with two-piece canning lids for canning any food at home. [T:SLO-223,SLO-212]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): use this card only with jars that take two-piece canning lids; it gives no method for twist-off jars or jars with glass lids, so do not use them for this recipe. [T:SLO-223]

A sealed lid is concave, curved down slightly in the centre; if the centre is flat or bulging, the jar may not be sealed. [T:SLO-196]

If a jar has not sealed at this check, keep it in the refrigerator and eat it within several days. [T:SLO-197]

Store sealed jars in a clean, cool, dark, dry place, not above 95 °F (35 °C) and not near hot pipes, a stove or a furnace, in an uninsulated attic or in direct sunlight; in such conditions the food may spoil. [T:SLO-198]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): keep an opened jar in the refrigerator and use it within 1 week. [T:SLO-322]

## When to throw it out

Before using a stored jar, check that the lid is tight and holds a vacuum: a good seal has a concave centre. [T:SLO-191]

Look for streaks of dried food running from the top of the jar, rising air bubbles and unnatural colour. [T:SLO-191]

When opening the jar, check for an unnatural smell, spurting liquid and cotton-like mould on the food or under the lid. [T:SLO-191]

Do not taste food from a jar with an unsealed lid or with signs of spoilage. [T:SLO-190]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): throw out such a jar. [T:SLO-190]
