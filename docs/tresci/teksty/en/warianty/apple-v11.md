# Apple scrap jelly from peels and cores (UC Master Food Preservers)

This card describes the University of California Master Food Preserver Program (UC MFP) recipe for apple scrap jelly: juice from apple peels and cores boiled with powdered pectin and sugar, filled into sterile jars and processed in a boiling-water bath. [T:SLO-800,SLO-802,SLO-803]

## !

- NCHFP keeps its apple recipes but advises using fresh, sound apples, and if you are unsure of their pH, refrigerate or freeze apple products instead of storing them at room temperature. [T:SLO-048]
- Do not reduce the sugar: NCHFP warns that in traditional recipes too little sugar prevents gelling and may allow yeasts and molds to grow. [T:SLO-001]

## What this card covers

The UC MFP sheet is adapted from the Ball recipe website and NCHFP; it does not describe a test of its own. [T:SLO-804]

This card uses only the peels and cores, as the recipe does; do not add other fruit or juice. [T:SLO-800]

## Ingredients

A US cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

The recipe uses 4 1/2 cups of juice made from apple peels and cores, 5 cups of sugar and 1 box of powdered pectin, and makes about 6 half-pint jars. [T:SLO-800]

Use peels and cores from fresh apples, free from any signs of decay or mold. [T:SLO-048]

NCHFP warns not to reduce the sugar in traditional recipes: too little sugar prevents gelling and may allow yeasts and molds to grow. [T:SLO-001]

## Making the juice

Put the peels and cores in a pan, add cold water, up to 1 cup per pound (about 450 g) of fruit, and bring to a boil. [T:SLO-801]

Simmer for 15 minutes and no longer: overcooking destroys the pectin. [T:SLO-801]

Let the juice drip through a damp jelly bag; clear jelly comes from juice that drips without pressing or squeezing. [T:SLO-801,ACI-482]

## Preparing the jars

The recipe sterilizes the jars by boiling them for 10 minutes below 1000 ft and adds 1 minute for each further 1000 ft. [T:SLO-802]

NCHFP puts the empty jars right side up on the rack in a boiling-water canner and fills the canner and jars with hot (not boiling) water to 1 inch (about 2.5 cm) above the tops of the jars. [T:SLO-120]

To sterilize empty jars, NCHFP boils them for 10 minutes below 1000 ft, and above that 1 minute longer for each additional thousand feet. [T:SLO-118]

JarAtlas rule for metres: boil the jars for 10 minutes up to 300 m, and add 1 minute for every further 300 m or part of it. [T:SLO-119]

## Making the jelly

Bring the juice with the pectin to a full boil, stir in the sugar, return to a boil over high heat and boil hard for 1 minute, stirring constantly. [T:SLO-802]

## Canning

Just before filling, remove and drain the hot sterilized jars one at a time. [T:SLO-120]

Ladle the hot jelly into the hot, sterile jars, leaving 1/4 inch (about 0.6 cm) of headspace. [T:SLO-802,SLO-003]

Do not seal the jars with paraffin or wax: NCHFP no longer recommends paraffin or wax seals for any sweet spread, because of possible mold contamination. [T:SLO-002]

Up to 1000 ft (up to 300 m) process the jars in a boiling-water bath for 5 minutes. [T:SLO-803,ACI-176]

If the jars are not sterilized, the recipe processes the filled jars for 10 minutes. [T:SLO-803]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): use sterile jars at every elevation. [T:SLO-803,ACI-626]

The recipe adds 1 minute for each 1000 ft above sea level; JarAtlas uses the longer Sure-Jell altitude table below instead. [T:SLO-803]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): above 1000 ft add the Sure-Jell altitude increases to these 5 minutes: add 5 minutes at 1001–3000 ft (above 300 m up to 900 m), 10 minutes at 3001–6000 ft (above 900 m up to 1800 m), 15 minutes at 6001–8000 ft (above 1800 m up to 2400 m) and 20 minutes at 8001–10000 ft (above 2400 m up to 3000 m). [T:ACI-732,ACI-176,SLO-209,ACI-930,SLO-803]

Above 10000 ft (above 3000 m) Sure-Jell gives no time, so this card does not cover canning at that elevation. [T:ACI-732,ACI-930]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 3000 ft becomes 900 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

8000 ft (2438.4 m) becomes 2400 m and 10000 ft (3048 m) becomes 3000 m, so the range without a time is wider than in the source. [T:SLO-209,ACI-930]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

NCHFP gives times for jams and jellies made with packaged pectin only for half-pint and pint jars; JarAtlas rule: use only these jars for this recipe. [T:ACI-609]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): a pint holds about 473 ml, so a 0.5 l jar has no tested time in this recipe; use jars of up to 473 ml. [T:ACI-179,ACI-174]

## Cooling and storing

When you take the hot jars out of the canner, do not retighten the lids or bands; this can cut through the gasket and cause seal failures. [T:SLO-210]

Cool the jars at room temperature for 12 to 24 hours, then remove only the screw bands (the metal rings of two-piece lids), leaving the flat lids on the jars, and test the seal: press the middle of the lid, and if it springs up when you release it, the jar is not sealed. [T:SLO-196,SLO-212]

NCHFP calls Mason-type canning jars with self-sealing two-piece lids (a flat lid and a screw band) the best choice, and this seal test and the method on this card assume them; NCHFP does not recommend other commercial jars whose mouths cannot be sealed with two-piece canning lids for canning any food at home. [T:SLO-223,SLO-212]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): use this card only with jars that take two-piece canning lids; it gives no method for twist-off jars or jars with glass lids, so do not use them for this recipe. [T:SLO-223]

A sealed lid is concave, curved down slightly in the centre; if the centre is flat or bulging, the jar may not be sealed. [T:SLO-196]

If a jar has not sealed at this check, keep it in the refrigerator and eat it within several days. [T:SLO-197]

Store sealed jars in a clean, cool, dark, dry place, not above 95 °F (35 °C) and not near hot pipes, a stove or a furnace, in an uninsulated attic or in direct sunlight; in such conditions the food may spoil. [T:SLO-198]

Keep opened jelly in the refrigerator at 40 °F (about 4 °C) or lower. [T:SLO-018]

Regular full-sugar cooked jellies made with added pectin, like this one, are best kept in the refrigerator for 1 month after opening. [T:SLO-018]

Opening the jar often or leaving it out at room temperature for long periods shortens this time; you can spoon the amount you need into a bowl and put the jar back in the refrigerator quickly. [T:SLO-213]

Keep the opened jar in the refrigerator until it is used up. [T:SLO-213]

## When to throw it out

Before using a stored jar, check that the lid is tight and holds a vacuum: a good seal has a concave centre. [T:SLO-191]

Look for streaks of dried food running from the top of the jar, rising air bubbles and unnatural colour. [T:SLO-191]

When opening the jar, check for an unnatural smell, spurting liquid and cotton-like mould on the food or under the lid. [T:SLO-191]

Do not taste food from a jar with an unsealed lid or with signs of spoilage. [T:SLO-190]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): throw out such a jar. [T:SLO-190]

Discard jelly with mold on it; USDA and microbiologists advise against scooping out the mold and eating the rest. [T:SLO-012]

Check an opened jar regularly for signs of spoilage such as mold, yeast or an off odor, including a fermented, yeasty or alcohol smell; if you find any, throw out the whole jar. [T:SLO-181,SLO-013]
