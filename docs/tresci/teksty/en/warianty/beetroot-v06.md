# Pickled beets (NCHFP)

This card describes NCHFP pickled beets: cooked beets in a vinegar, sugar and water brine, hot packed and processed in a boiling-water bath. [T:ACI-212,ACI-214,ACI-216]

## Why the recipe must not be changed

Beets are not an acid food: their natural pH is about 5.30–6.60. [T:ACI-104]

Rules in the USA count as acid foods only those with a natural pH of 4.6 or below. [T:FER-001]

The whole product needs a minimum, uniform level of acid to prevent the growth of botulism bacteria. [T:ACI-017]

Do not change the proportions of vinegar, vegetables and water, and do not use vinegar of unknown acidity; use only recipes with tested proportions. [T:ACI-016]

For pickles, white distilled vinegar and cider vinegar with 5% acidity are recommended. [T:ACI-018]

Penn State Extension advises vinegar of 5–7% acidity for home food preservation and advises against diluting vinegar with water unless a research-tested recipe says so. [T:ACI-209,ACI-210]

JarAtlas rule (cautious, for readers outside the USA): use vinegar labelled 5% or 6% acidity; do not use 10% vinegar and do not dilute it with water yourself. [T:ACI-211]

## Ingredients

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

For about 8 pint jars NCHFP uses 7 pounds (about 3.2 kg) of beets 2 to 2 1/2 inches (about 5–6.4 cm) in diameter, 4 cups of vinegar with 5% acidity, 1 1/2 teaspoons of canning or pickling salt, 2 cups of sugar and 2 cups of water. [T:ACI-212]

Spices: 2 cinnamon sticks and 12 whole cloves; 4 to 6 onions 2 to 2 1/2 inches in diameter are optional. [T:ACI-212]

## Preparing the beets

Trim off the beet tops, leaving 1 inch (about 2.5 cm) of stem and the roots, so the beets do not bleed color. [T:ACI-213]

Wash the beets thoroughly and sort them by size. [T:ACI-213]

Cover beets of similar size with boiling water and cook until tender, about 25–30 minutes. [T:ACI-213]

Drain and discard the cooking liquid, then cool the beets. [T:ACI-213]

Trim off the roots and stems, slip off the skins and cut the beets into slices 1/4 inch (about 0.6 cm) thick. [T:ACI-213]

Peel and thinly slice the onions. [T:ACI-213]

## Brine and filling

Combine the vinegar, salt, sugar and fresh water. [T:ACI-214]

Put the spices in a cheesecloth bag, add it to the vinegar mixture and bring to a boil. [T:ACI-214]

Add the beets and onions, simmer for 5 minutes and remove the spice bag. [T:ACI-214]

Fill the jars with beets and onions, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:ACI-215]

Add the hot vinegar solution, again leaving 1/2 inch of headspace. [T:ACI-215]

Put on the lids and process. [T:ACI-215]

## Whole baby beets

NCHFP also allows whole baby beets 1 to 1 1/2 inches (about 2.5–3.8 cm) in diameter: follow the same directions, but pack them whole without slicing; the onions may be left out. [T:ACI-217]

## Processing

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process pint and quart jars in a boiling-water bath for 30 minutes. [T:ACI-216,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–3000 ft (above 300 m up to 900 m) process them for 35 minutes. [T:ACI-225,ACI-176]

At 3001–6000 ft (above 900 m up to 1800 m) process them for 40 minutes. [T:ACI-226,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 45 minutes. [T:ACI-227,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 3000 ft becomes 900 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

NCHFP times are for US jar sizes: a pint holds about 473 ml and a quart about 946 ml. [T:ACI-174]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): this recipe has one time for pints and quarts, so it applies to jars of up to 946 ml, for example 0.5 l or 0.9 l. [T:ACI-175,ACI-216]

This NCHFP recipe gives no time for jars larger than a quart, such as 1 l, so JarAtlas recommends smaller jars. [T:ACI-175]
