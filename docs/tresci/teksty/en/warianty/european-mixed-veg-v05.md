# Refrigerator cucumber pickle with onion or peppers (Wisconsin Extension)

This card describes the Wisconsin Extension recipe for cucumber pickles with onions, in which peppers may replace the onion: a refrigerator pickle kept for up to 2 weeks, not canned. [T:ACI-503,ACI-504]

## Ingredients

8 cups thinly sliced cucumbers and 1 cup sliced onion, or in place of the onion an equal amount of other vegetables such as cauliflower, peppers or carrots. [T:ACI-503]

Pickling solution: 4 cups vinegar (5% acetic acid), 2 cups sugar, 1/3 cup canning and pickling salt, and 1 1/2 teaspoons each of celery seed, mustard seed and turmeric. [T:ACI-503,ACI-709]

This recipe uses vinegar without water; do not add water. [T:ACI-503,ACI-016]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): if you use more than one vegetable besides the cucumbers, for example onion and peppers, keep their combined amount at 1 cup. [T:ACI-503,ACI-016]

Use only vinegar labelled 5% acidity; white and cider vinegar can be used interchangeably; do not use wine vinegars, homemade vinegar or lemon juice. [T:ACI-509]

Vinegar with less than 5% acidity should never be used in home food preservation. [T:ACI-516]

Do not change the proportions of vinegar, vegetables and water in the recipe. [T:ACI-016]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): do not replace it with a stronger vinegar and do not dilute a stronger vinegar with water yourself; Penn State Extension advises staying within 5–7% acidity for home food preservation and diluting vinegar only when a tested recipe says so. [T:ACI-209,ACI-210]

## Preparing

Wash the jars in hot, soapy water, rinse them well and sterilize them by boiling for 10 minutes. [T:ACI-709]

Mix the pickling solution until the sugar dissolves; heating is not required, but it dissolves the sugar and pickles the vegetables faster. [T:ACI-709]

Wash and prepare the vegetables; trim the blossom ends from the cucumbers and discard them. [T:ACI-709]

Fill hot sterilized pint or quart jars with the vegetables, cover them with hot pickling solution and cap the jars with clean lids. [T:ACI-709]

The recipe yields 2 quarts. [T:ACI-709]

## Storing

Label and date the jars and store them in the refrigerator at 40 °F (4.4 °C) or colder for up to 2 weeks. [T:ACI-504]

These pickles are not heat processed, so they are not shelf-stable at room temperature and must be kept in the refrigerator. [T:ACI-501,ACI-022]

Do not decrease the amount of vinegar, because an unsafe product may result; for a milder taste add sugar rather than reduce the vinegar. [T:ACI-501]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): keep the jar on the middle shelf of the refrigerator, not in the door: Wisconsin Extension assumes 40 °F (4.4 °C) or colder for refrigerator pickles, while a Polish sanitary inspection office gives about 10 °C for the door, even though it suggests the door for jars of preserves. [T:ACI-523]

## Why the vinegar matters

Fresh vegetables are low-acid foods, with a pH above 4.6. [T:ACI-005]

The acid level must be adequate and even throughout the product to prevent the growth of botulism bacteria. [T:ACI-017]
