# Gravlax (UCCE)

This card describes gravlax, raw salmon cured in the refrigerator with salt, sugar, dill and a spirit, following the Vodka-Dill-Citrus Gravlax recipe in a UCCE Master Food Preservers workshop handout, which they adapted from a cooking blog; the safety rules on this card are UCCE’s own and the FDA’s, plus JarAtlas rules marked as such. [T:MIE-148,MIE-056,MIE-057]

## !

- According to UCCE, salmon for gravlax must be frozen before curing, because salt and acid do not kill parasites. [T:MIE-056]
- According to UCCE, most home freezers cannot reach the temperatures needed, so use only commercially frozen salmon. [T:MIE-057]

## Who should be careful

Gravlax is not a cooked product. [T:MIE-149]

According to the FDA, freezing kills parasites but not all harmful germs, so cooking seafood is the safest choice. [T:SLO-433]

According to the FDA, pregnant women, children, older adults and people with weakened immune systems should avoid raw or undercooked fish. [T:SLO-820]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): do not serve this gravlax to them. [T:SLO-820]

## The salmon

Use a skin-on salmon fillet that was commercially frozen and then thawed. [T:MIE-148]

According to the FDA, freezing and storing at −20 °C (−4 °F) or below for 7 days in total kills parasites in fish. [T:MIE-046]

According to the FDA, brining and pickling reduce the parasite hazard but do not eliminate it. [T:MIE-049]

Choose a piece no bigger than about 2 1/2 pounds and no thicker than about 1 1/2 inches. [T:MIE-148]

Thaw the salmon completely in the refrigerator, because the cure cannot get into frozen flesh. [T:MIE-149]

Do not wash the raw fish; pat it dry with paper towels and remove the pin bones. [T:MIE-149]

## Ingredients

UCCE gives these amounts for 1 pound of skin-on salmon fillet. [T:MIE-148]

4 tablespoons coarse salt (the recipe uses pink Himalayan sea salt), 3 tablespoons sugar and 1/2 teaspoon freshly cracked peppercorns. [T:MIE-148]

1/2 cup chopped fresh dill and the zest of 1 lemon and 1 orange. [T:MIE-148]

2 tablespoons vodka, gin or aquavit; the spirit is not needed for curing and can be left out. [T:MIE-148]

Use salt without iodine or other additives such as anticaking agents. [T:MIE-148]

For a bigger piece, prorate all the ingredients; use at least 2 tablespoons of salt per pound of salmon, for example 3 tablespoons for 1 1/2 pounds. [T:MIE-148]

## Curing

Mix the dry ingredients. [T:MIE-149]

Lay the salmon skin-side down on plastic wrap, drizzle it with the spirit and rub the mixture evenly over the top and bottom. [T:MIE-149]

Wrap it tightly, then wrap it again in a second piece of plastic wrap. [T:MIE-149]

Put the package skin-side down in a dish, cover it with a plate and weight it. [T:MIE-149]

Cure the salmon in the refrigerator. [T:MIE-149]

After 12–24 hours, turn it over, put the weights back and keep turning it at least every 24 hours until it is cured, which takes 1–3 days; this recipe is best after 2–3 days. [T:MIE-149]

Cured salmon feels firm at the thickest part, not raw or soft. [T:MIE-149]

Scrape off the seasonings, pat the fish dry and slice it thinly. [T:MIE-149]

## Storing

Keep finished gravlax in the refrigerator, tightly wrapped, for up to 5–7 days. [T:MIE-058]

Freezing finished gravlax harms its texture. [T:MIE-149]

## When to throw it away

If it smells off, it probably is; when in doubt, throw it out. [T:MIE-149]

Throw away gravlax that has been in the refrigerator for more than 7 days. [T:MIE-058]

Do not keep gravlax out of the refrigerator for more than 2 hours, or for more than 1 hour when the temperature is above 90 °F (about 32 °C). [T:SLO-408]
