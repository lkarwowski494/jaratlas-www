# Makdous: why there is no recipe here

There is no recipe here for makdous, small Middle Eastern eggplants with nuts and peppers kept in oil. [T:-]

## !

- Covering foods in oil may create the oxygen-free conditions needed for botulism toxin; the CDC says that preparing roasted vegetables in oil at home may be hazardous, especially if such foods stay above refrigerator temperature. [T:OLE-088]
- There are no validated processes for vegetables and herbs in oil made at home; in the USA, the rules for such products apply only to commercial processors. [T:OLE-028]
- Vegetables in oil, including partly dried ones, give C. botulinum good conditions to grow (no oxygen, the pH and the water in the vegetables), and at room temperature the bacterium can multiply quickly. [T:OLE-072]
- Appearance, smell and taste cannot tell you whether vegetables in oil carry a botulism hazard. [T:OLE-027]

## Why

The BfR names eggplant among the vegetables in oil with which a household cannot make sure that C. botulinum will not grow and form toxin. [T:OLE-076]

Peppers, garlic, mushrooms and eggplant have been involved in botulism outbreaks from vegetables and herbs in oil, mostly homemade products. [T:OLE-024]

In Italy in 1993, two type B botulism outbreaks were linked to commercially prepared roasted eggplant in oil. [T:OLE-025]

JarAtlas has found no tested recipe for makdous from a checked source. [T:-]
