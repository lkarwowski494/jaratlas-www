# Syrup-blanched dried apricots (Clemson HGIC)

This card describes how to dry apricot halves after blanching them in syrup, following Clemson University Home & Garden Information Center (HGIC); the product is similar to candied fruit. [T:SUS-322,SUS-420]

## Choosing and preparing

Wash the fruit thoroughly and sort it: discard any fruit that shows decay, bruises or mould, because such defects can affect all of the dried product. [T:SUS-223]

Pit the apricots and cut them in half; you may also slice them. [T:SUS-322]

## Blanching in syrup

Blanching fruit in syrup helps it keep its colour during drying and storage. [T:SUS-420]

Clemson HGIC blanches apricots in syrup for 10 minutes. [T:SUS-322]

A US cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

Bring 1 cup of sugar, 1 cup of light corn syrup and 2 cups of water to a boil in a pot. [T:SUS-420]

Add 1 pound (about 450 g) of prepared fruit and simmer for 10 minutes. [T:SUS-420]

Remove the pot from the heat and let the fruit stand in the hot syrup for 30 minutes. [T:SUS-420]

Lift the fruit out of the syrup, rinse it lightly in cold water, drain it on paper towels and place it on the dryer trays. [T:SUS-420]

This card gives no substitutes for the syrup ingredients and no other amounts of fruit per batch of syrup. [T:-]

## Drying

Arrange the fruit on trays in a single layer, pit cavity up; stir and turn large pieces every 3–4 hours. [T:SUS-074]

Dry the fruit at 140 °F (60 °C) in a dehydrator or oven. [T:SUS-001]

Do not use trays or screens made of galvanized mesh; also avoid copper and aluminium mesh. [T:SUS-008]

Only use an oven if it can be set as low as 140 °F (60 °C); if it cannot go that low, the fruit cooks instead of drying. [T:SUS-069]

In an oven, keep 140–150 °F (about 60–66 °C): place a calibrated oven thermometer on the rack or tray and check it every 2 hours throughout drying. [T:SUS-070]

Leave the oven door open 2–3 inches (about 5–7.5 cm) so moisture can escape. [T:SUS-071]

In a conventional oven, drying can take up to twice as long as in a dehydrator, depending on air circulation. [T:SUS-072]

Fruit scorches easily near the end of drying: when drying is almost done, turn the oven heat off and open the door wide for one more hour. [T:SUS-073]

In a dehydrator, apricot halves dry in 24–36 hours; slices dry faster. [T:SUS-322]

## Testing for dryness and conditioning

Before testing for dryness, take out a few pieces and let them cool to room temperature: warm fruit seems softer, moister and more pliable than it really is. [T:SUS-062]

The fruit is dry when, after you squeeze a handful, no moisture is left on your hand and the pieces spring apart when released; cut cooled pieces show no visible moisture and none can be squeezed out. [T:SUS-063]

Home dried fruit should contain about 20% moisture. [T:SUS-016]

After drying, cool the fruit for 30–60 minutes before packing; do not wait too long to pack it, because moisture could get back into it. [T:SUS-068]

Pack only completely cooled fruit: warm dried food sweats, and that moisture can be enough for mould to grow. [T:SUS-024]

Condition the cooled fruit: pack it loosely in closed jars for 7–10 days and shake the jars daily. [T:SUS-018]

If condensation develops in a jar, return the fruit to the dehydrator. [T:SUS-019]

## Packing and storing

Store the dried fruit in clean, dry canning jars, plastic freezer containers with tight-fitting lids or plastic freezer bags. [T:SUS-064]

Packaging must be food grade: do not use trash bags or plastic or fibreboard containers that previously held non-food items. [T:SUS-065]

Pack amounts that can be used at one time: each opening exposes the dried fruit to air and moisture, which lowers quality and can lead to spoilage. [T:SUS-067]

Vacuum packaging is not a preservation method by itself: removing oxygen increases the risk of botulism, and a product that needs refrigeration still needs it. [T:SUS-025]

Store the dried fruit in a cool, dry, dark place. [T:SUS-066]

Most dried fruits can be stored for 1 year at 60 °F (about 16 °C) and 6 months at 80 °F (about 27 °C); the warmer, the shorter. [T:SUS-020]

## When to throw away

Check the dried fruit often (glass shows condensation); if it has taken up moisture but is not spoiled, use it at once or dry it again and repack it. [T:SUS-023]

Throw away mouldy dried fruit; also throw away dried fruit that smells off. [T:SUS-022]
