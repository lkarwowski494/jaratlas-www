# Quick refrigerator cucumber and onion pickles (Mississippi State Extension)

This card describes the Mississippi State Extension recipe for quick-pickle cucumbers and onions, a refrigerator pickle that keeps for about a week. [T:ACI-510]

## Ingredients

2 large cucumbers, 1 medium onion, 1 garlic clove, 1 cup white distilled or cider vinegar and 1 cup water. [T:ACI-510]

The brine also has 2 tablespoons sugar, 2 teaspoons salt and 1 teaspoon pepper. [T:ACI-708]

Use only vinegar labelled 5% acidity; white and cider vinegar can be used interchangeably; do not use wine vinegars, homemade vinegar or lemon juice. [T:ACI-509]

Vinegar with less than 5% acidity should never be used in home food preservation. [T:ACI-516]

Do not change the proportions of vinegar, vegetables and water in the recipe. [T:ACI-016]

Do not use vinegar stronger than the 5–7% range Penn State Extension advises for home food preservation, and do not dilute a stronger vinegar with water yourself. [T:ACI-209,ACI-210]

## Preparing

Wash the cucumbers under running water and slice them thinly. [T:ACI-708]

Peel the onion and cut it into thin slices; cut the garlic into small pieces. [T:ACI-708]

Place the vegetables in a one-quart jar (about 946 ml). [T:ACI-708]

Stir the vinegar, water, sugar, salt and pepper until dissolved, then pour the brine into the jar, making sure the vegetables are covered. [T:ACI-708,ACI-510]

Cover the jar and refrigerate until chilled, or overnight. [T:ACI-708]

## Storing

The vegetables keep for about a week in the refrigerator. [T:ACI-510]

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. [T:ACI-708]

These pickles are not heat processed, so they are not shelf-stable at room temperature and must be kept in the refrigerator. [T:ACI-501,ACI-022]

Do not decrease the amount of vinegar, because an unsafe product may result; for a milder taste add sugar rather than reduce the vinegar. [T:ACI-501]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): keep the jar on the middle shelf of the refrigerator, not in the door: US extension guidance (for example Wisconsin Extension) assumes 40 °F (4.4 °C) or colder for refrigerator pickles, while a Polish sanitary inspection office gives about 10 °C for the door, even though it suggests the door for jars of preserves. [T:ACI-523]

## Why the vinegar matters

Fresh vegetables are low-acid foods, with a pH above 4.6. [T:ACI-005]

The acid level must be adequate and even throughout the product to prevent the growth of botulism bacteria. [T:ACI-017]
