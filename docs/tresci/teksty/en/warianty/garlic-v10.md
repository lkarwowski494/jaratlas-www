# Roasted garlic in olive oil, frozen

This card gives no recipe for roasting garlic; it describes how to store roasted garlic in olive oil: freeze it or keep it in the refrigerator for no more than 4 days. [T:OLE-002,OLE-006,OLE-029]

## !

- Do not store home-made oils with unacidified garlic or herbs at room temperature: keep them in the refrigerator for no more than 2–4 days, or freeze them. [T:OLE-019]
- Without freezing, keep garlic in oil in the refrigerator at 40 °F (4.4 °C) or lower for no more than 4 days. [T:OLE-002]

## Preparing

The same botulism hazard at room temperature as for garlic in oil exists for roasted garlic stored in oil. [T:OLE-006]

Do not store garlic in oil at room temperature: low acidity, no free oxygen in the oil and warmth are ideal conditions for Clostridium botulinum to grow and produce toxin; the same applies to other low-acid foods in oil, such as herbs and chili peppers. [T:OLE-053]

Roasting does not remove this hazard: even boiling does not sterilize, because Clostridium botulinum spores survive boiling water. [T:OLE-006,OLE-048]

The safest recommendation for vegetables and herbs in oil is to freeze them. [T:OLE-029]

Keeping garlic in oil in the refrigerator for longer than 4 days may allow Clostridium botulinum to grow. [T:OLE-004]

## Packaging

Package garlic in oil in glass freezer jars or plastic freezer boxes, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) headspace, then label, date and freeze. [T:OLE-051]

If you use glass, choose wide-mouth jars made for freezing and canning, because regular glass jars break easily at freezer temperatures. [T:SLO-251]

According to Oregon State University Extension, crushed vegetables and herbs in oil can be frozen in ice cube trays and then packed in airtight containers as ready seasoning portions. [T:OLE-056]

According to the same publication, pack frozen flavored oils airtight, for example in small glass or plastic jars, so their aroma does not penetrate other foods in the freezer. [T:OLE-056]

Label garlic in oil kept in the refrigerator with the preparation date and preferably also a “discard after” date, that is 4 days later. [T:OLE-052]

## Freezing and storage

Freeze at 0 °F (−18 °C) or lower. [T:SUS-050]

To speed up freezing, set the freezer to −10 °F (about −23 °C) or lower about 24 hours in advance. [T:SUS-051]

Do not overload the freezer: add only as much unfrozen food as will freeze within 24 hours. [T:SUS-052]

Keep the freezer at 0 °F (−18 °C) or lower, because at higher temperatures food loses quality faster; keep a freezer thermometer in it and check it often. [T:SLO-255]

According to the FDA, freezing does not kill most bacteria; it only stops them from growing. [T:OLE-057]

According to the NCHFP, garlic in oil can be kept in the freezer for up to several months. [T:OLE-003]

Use the oldest packages first. [T:SLO-261]

## Thawing and use

JarAtlas rule (cautious): the sources we read do not say how long garlic in oil can be kept after thawing, so thaw it only in the refrigerator and only as much as you will use right away. [T:SLO-303]

Do not keep thawed leftovers; throw them away. [T:SLO-303]

Never thaw food at room temperature or in warm water: its outer layer then warms into the danger zone of 40–140 °F (about 4–60 °C), where bacteria multiply rapidly. [T:SLO-258]

Keep the refrigerator at 40 °F (4 °C) or lower. [T:OLE-057]

## When to throw it away

Food is safe while it stays frozen, but once it thaws and warms above 40 °F (about 4 °C), bacteria present before freezing can start to multiply. [T:SUS-059]

During a power outage keep the freezer closed: according to the FDA, a full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours, a half-full one for 24 hours. [T:SLO-259]

Appearance, smell and taste cannot tell you whether vegetables in oil carry a botulism hazard. [T:OLE-027]

JarAtlas rule (cautious) after a freezer failure: throw away any package that has no ice crystals and is above 40 °F (about 4 °C), or that you cannot check. [T:SLO-260]

The FDA also says to throw away perishable food that has been at room temperature for 2 hours or more, or 1 hour when it is above 90 °F (about 32 °C) outside. [T:SLO-260]
