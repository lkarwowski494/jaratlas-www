# Napa cabbage kimchi with Asian pear (CSU recipe)

This card describes the Colorado State University (CSU) Extension recipe for baechu kimchi with the Asian pear, which is optional in that recipe. [T:FER-066,FER-115]

## !

- For safety, kimchi should be stored in the refrigerator. [T:FER-061]
- Discard kimchi that shows mold, an unpleasant odor different from the normal fermentation smell, or an unusual texture. [T:FER-057]

## What this card covers

The CSU recipe lists 1 Asian pear as an optional ingredient. [T:FER-111]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): use no more pear than the 1 Asian pear the recipe lists, and do not add other fruit or ingredients outside the recipe, because the recipe does not test them. [T:FER-115]

## Ingredients

CSU uses 2 medium heads of napa cabbage, 6–8 pounds in total, and 1 1/2 cups of coarse non-iodized salt, divided. [T:FER-066]

The other ingredients are 1 gallon plus 1/2 cup of cold water, divided, and 2 tablespoons of sweet rice flour. [T:FER-111]

Seasonings: 1–10 cloves of garlic to taste, about 3 slices of fresh ginger root (about 0.2–0.4 oz) and 1 cup of Korean red pepper powder. [T:FER-111]

Vegetables: 1/2 Korean radish (about 1–1.5 pounds) or daikon, 10 green onions and 1 Asian pear. [T:FER-111]

Optional: 1 teaspoon of fish sauce, and 2 teaspoons of finely ground salt added only as needed. [T:FER-111]

## Salting the cabbage

Rinse the cabbage heads under cold water, drain them and cut away any spoiled or damaged spots. [T:FER-112]

Cut each head into quarters, remove the core and chop the quarters into 2-inch pieces. [T:FER-112]

Dip the pieces briefly in a solution of 1/2 cup of coarse non-iodized salt in 1 gallon of cold water, then discard the solution. [T:FER-112]

Sprinkle the cabbage with 1 cup of coarse non-iodized salt, massage it in and leave it covered at room temperature for 3–6 hours. [T:FER-112]

Rinse the cabbage 3–4 times with cold water and drain it in a colander for at least 30 minutes. [T:FER-112]

## Seasoning and packing

Bring the sweet rice flour and 1/2 cup of water to a boil and let it cool. [T:FER-113]

Finely mince the garlic and ginger and mix them with the cooled paste and the red pepper powder. [T:FER-113]

Clean and peel the radish and the Asian pear, clean and trim the green onions, and cut them all into matchsticks about 1 inch long. [T:FER-113]

Mix the paste and the vegetables with gloved hands, add the fish sauce, and add salt only as needed. [T:FER-113]

Combine the cabbage with this paste and mix thoroughly. [T:FER-113]

Pack the kimchi tightly into a container filled only about 2/3 full, because fermentation releases carbon dioxide. [T:FER-113]

Do not use metal containers or earthenware with cracks or chips. [T:FER-113]

Cover tightly, sealing jars finger-tip tight, and set the container on a plate or in a bowl to catch any overflow. [T:FER-113]

## Fermenting

Option 1: put the kimchi in the refrigerator, where it ferments slowly over 3–4 days; CSU says this may be preferred, especially in hot weather. [T:FER-114]

Option 2: keep the sealed container in a well-ventilated place at a fairly constant room temperature, ideally around 68 °F (20 °C), for only 1–2 days, tasting it daily. [T:FER-114]

According to CSU, the final salt concentration of kimchi is 2–5% and its optimum pH is about 4.2. [T:FER-061]

According to UGA Extension, after about 2 weeks fermentation makes kimchi acidic enough, with a pH below 4.6, to inhibit most foodborne pathogens; early in fermentation the pH may still be too high. [T:FER-063]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): this does not mean fermenting kimchi for 2 weeks at room temperature; after the 1–2 days of option 2, keep it in the refrigerator. [T:FER-114,FER-062]

## Storing

Store the kimchi tightly covered in the refrigerator, pressed down to minimize air exposure; it may become more sour over time. [T:FER-114]

UGA Extension says that keeping kimchi at 41 °F (5 °C) or below helps ensure its safety and quality. [T:FER-062]

CSU says kimchi is best eaten within a week, because its quality deteriorates with longer fermentation. [T:FER-061]

Other Extension sources give longer times, up to 6 months for a recipe reprinted by Oregon State University; they all agree that kimchi belongs in the refrigerator and that its quality declines over time. [T:FER-067]

## When to throw it out

According to CSU, discard the kimchi if you see signs of surface mold. [T:FER-057]

According to UGA Extension, mold, an unpleasant odor different from the normal fermentation smell, or an unusual texture are signs of spoilage, and the kimchi should then be discarded. [T:FER-057]

## Who should be careful

Kimchi can contain allergens such as fish sauce, shrimp paste, salted anchovies, sesame oil, soy sauce or nuts. [T:FER-064]
