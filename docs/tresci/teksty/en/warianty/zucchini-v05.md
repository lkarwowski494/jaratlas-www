# Bread-and-butter zucchini pickles (NCHFP)

This card describes the NCHFP recipe for pickled bread-and-butter zucchini: zucchini and onions simmered in a vinegar, sugar and spice brine, hot packed and processed in a boiling-water bath. [T:ACI-253,ACI-255,ACI-256]

## Why you must not change the recipe

Zucchini is a low-acid food with a pH above 4.6. [T:ACI-026]

The FDA list gives cooked zucchini a pH of about 5.69–6.10. [T:ACI-086]

Canning zucchini without pickling it is not recommended; USDA withdrew its earlier directions because the processing times were uncertain. [T:SZY-026]

The whole product must contain a minimum, uniform level of acid so that botulism bacteria cannot grow. [T:ACI-017]

Do not change the proportions of vinegar, vegetables and water and do not use vinegar of unknown acidity; use only recipes with tested proportions. [T:ACI-016]

White distilled vinegar and cider vinegar of 5% acidity are recommended for pickles. [T:ACI-018]

Penn State Extension advises vinegar of 5–7% acidity for home food preservation and advises against diluting vinegar with water unless a research-tested recipe says so. [T:ACI-209,ACI-210]

JarAtlas rule (cautious, for readers outside the USA): use white distilled (spirit) vinegar or cider vinegar labelled 5% or 6% acidity; do not use 10% vinegar and do not dilute it with water to make 5% vinegar; add only the water the recipe lists. [T:ACI-211,ACI-018]

## Ingredients

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

For about 8 to 9 pint jars NCHFP lists 16 cups of sliced fresh zucchini, 4 cups of thinly sliced onions, 1/2 cup of canning or pickling salt, 4 cups of white vinegar (5%) and 2 cups of sugar. [T:ACI-253]

Spices: 4 tablespoons of mustard seed, 2 tablespoons of celery seed and 2 teaspoons of ground turmeric. [T:ACI-253]

## Brining and filling

Cover the zucchini and onion slices with 1 inch (about 2.5 cm) of water and the salt. [T:ACI-254]

Let them stand for 2 hours and drain thoroughly. [T:ACI-254]

Combine the vinegar, sugar and spices and bring to a boil. [T:ACI-255]

Add the zucchini and onions and simmer for 5 minutes. [T:ACI-255]

Fill the jars with the vegetables and the pickling solution, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:ACI-255]

Put on the lids and process. [T:ACI-255]

## Processing

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process pints and quarts in a boiling-water bath for 10 minutes. [T:ACI-256,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–6000 ft (above 300 m up to 1800 m) process them for 15 minutes. [T:ACI-257,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 20 minutes. [T:ACI-258,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

NCHFP times are for US jar sizes: a pint holds about 473 ml and a quart about 946 ml. [T:ACI-174]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): this recipe has one time for pints and quarts, so it applies to jars of up to 946 ml, for example 0.5 l or 0.9 l. [T:ACI-175,ACI-256]

This NCHFP recipe gives no time for jars larger than a quart, such as 1 l, so JarAtlas recommends smaller jars. [T:ACI-175]
