# Passata: standard tomato sauce (NCHFP recipe)

This card describes NCHFP standard tomato sauce: sieved tomatoes cooked down, hot packed and processed in a boiling-water bath with added acid. [T:ACI-052,ACI-228]

## Why acid is needed

According to an FDA list, tomatoes have an approximate pH of 4.30–4.90, and the value varies widely with variety and conditions. [T:ACI-093]

Sugar may be added to balance the acidic taste, but the acid must not be left out. [T:ACI-047]

## Tomatoes

For a thin sauce NCHFP counts on an average of 5 pounds (about 2.3 kg) of tomatoes per quart jar, and for a thick sauce 6 1/2 pounds (about 2.9 kg). [T:ACI-234]

Caution (NCHFP): do not can tomatoes from dead or frost-killed vines. [T:ACI-049]

Wash the tomatoes, remove the stems and trim off bruised or discolored parts. [T:ACI-233]

Slice or quarter them into a large saucepan, crush them, heat them and simmer for 5 minutes. [T:ACI-233]

Press the hot tomatoes through a sieve or food mill to remove the skins and seeds. [T:ACI-233,ACI-228]

## Cooking down

Simmer the sieved tomatoes in a wide saucepan until the sauce is as thick as you want. [T:ACI-228]

For a thin sauce, boil until the volume is reduced by about 1/3; for a thick sauce, by about 1/2. [T:ACI-228]

## Acidifying

Add 2 tablespoons of bottled lemon juice or 1/2 teaspoon of citric acid to each quart jar (about 0.95 l). [T:ACI-044,ACI-229]

For pint jars, use 1 tablespoon of bottled lemon juice or 1/4 teaspoon of citric acid. [T:ACI-045]

JarAtlas rule for metric jars: to a jar larger than a pint, of up to 946 ml (for example 0.5 l or 0.9 l), add acid as for a quart: 2 tablespoons of bottled lemon juice or 1/2 teaspoon of citric acid. [T:ACI-193]

## Processing

Put the bottled lemon juice or citric acid in the jars and, if you like, add 1 teaspoon of salt per quart jar. [T:ACI-229]

Fill the jars with the hot sauce, leaving 1/4 inch (about 0.6 cm) of headspace, put on the lids and process. [T:ACI-229,ACI-052]

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) above sea level, process the sauce in a boiling-water bath for 35 minutes in pint jars and 40 minutes in quart jars. [T:ACI-052,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–3000 ft (above 300 m up to 900 m) process pints for 40 minutes and quarts for 45. [T:ACI-230,ACI-176]

At 3001–6000 ft (above 900 m up to 1800 m) process pints for 45 minutes and quarts for 50. [T:ACI-231,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process pints for 50 minutes and quarts for 55. [T:ACI-232,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 3000 ft becomes 900 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

NCHFP times are for US jar sizes: a pint holds about 473 ml and a quart about 946 ml. [T:ACI-174]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): for a jar of up to 473 ml use the pint time, and for a larger jar of up to 946 ml, for example 0.5 l or 0.9 l, use the quart time. [T:ACI-175]

This NCHFP recipe gives no time for jars larger than a quart, such as 1 l, so JarAtlas recommends smaller jars. [T:ACI-175]
