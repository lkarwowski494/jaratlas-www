# Quick refrigerator radish pickles with peppercorns (NC State method)

This card uses the NC State Extension and UC Master Food Preserver method for quick refrigerator pickles, with radishes and peppercorns; NC State lists radishes among good vegetables to start with. [T:ACI-505,ACI-706]

## Ingredients

Sour brine: 3 cups white vinegar (or cider vinegar), 3 cups water and 3 tablespoons canning or pickling salt; 2 tablespoons sugar are optional. [T:ACI-505,ACI-706]

Up to 2 tablespoons of fresh or dried herbs and spices may be added per jar; the suggested list includes peppercorns. [T:ACI-706]

Use only vinegar labelled 5% acidity; white and cider vinegar can be used interchangeably; do not use wine vinegars, homemade vinegar or lemon juice. [T:ACI-509]

Vinegar with less than 5% acidity should never be used in home food preservation. [T:ACI-516]

Do not change the proportions of vinegar, vegetables and water in the recipe. [T:ACI-016]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): do not replace it with a stronger vinegar and do not dilute a stronger vinegar with water yourself; Penn State Extension advises staying within 5–7% acidity for home food preservation and diluting vinegar only when a tested recipe says so. [T:ACI-209,ACI-210]

## Preparing

Wash the radishes and cut them into the desired shape; they do not need any cooking before pickling. [T:ACI-706]

Pack them snugly into washed pint-sized canning jars (about 473 ml) or similar glass or heatproof plastic containers with lids. [T:ACI-706]

Bring the brine to a boil and boil it for 2 minutes. [T:ACI-506]

Fill the jars with brine to within 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of the rim, put on the lids and refrigerate. [T:ACI-506]

## Storing

Let the flavor develop for 1–2 days before serving, and use the pickles within 2 weeks. [T:ACI-506]

These pickles are not heat processed, so they are not shelf-stable at room temperature and must be kept in the refrigerator. [T:ACI-501,ACI-022]

Do not decrease the amount of vinegar, because an unsafe product may result; for a milder taste add sugar rather than reduce the vinegar. [T:ACI-501]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): keep the jar on the middle shelf of the refrigerator, not in the door: Wisconsin Extension assumes 40 °F (4.4 °C) or colder for refrigerator pickles, while a Polish sanitary inspection office gives about 10 °C for the door, even though it suggests the door for jars of preserves. [T:ACI-523]

## Why the vinegar matters

Fresh vegetables are low-acid foods, with a pH above 4.6. [T:ACI-005]

The acid level must be adequate and even throughout the product to prevent the growth of botulism bacteria. [T:ACI-017]
