# Dried parasol mushroom caps: why there is no recipe here

There is no recipe here for drying parasol mushroom caps. [T:-]

## !

- The most frequent and most dangerous mushroom poisonings in Poland are caused by the death cap, which is mistaken for edible species such as the parasol mushroom. [T:SUS-141]
- Drying and cooking do not destroy the toxins of poisonous mushrooms, and only an expert can tell edible species from poisonous ones. [T:SUS-035]
- In Poland: if in doubt whether mushrooms are edible, take them fresh, before drying, to a sanitary-epidemiological station (sanepid); mushroom experts or classifiers there assess free of charge whether they are edible, inedible or poisonous. [T:SUS-142]

## Why

Only mushrooms that are certainly safe to eat are dried; anyone who is not an expert in mushroom identification should preserve only commercially sold mushrooms. [T:SUS-138]

Whole fruiting bodies, with cap and stem, are brought for assessment, because important identifying features are also on the stem; dried caps alone have no stem. [T:SUS-143]

In Poland, a mushroom expert (grzyboznawca) is authorised to assess whether fresh and dried mushrooms are fit to eat. [T:SUS-144]

Outside Poland this card does not name an institution that assesses mushrooms: if you are not sure a mushroom is edible, do not dry or eat it. [T:SUS-035,SUS-138]

## Symptoms of poisoning

With the most dangerous mushrooms, such as the death cap, the first symptoms often appear long after eating, sometimes only after several days, and after a temporary improvement the condition can worsen rapidly; with symptoms of poisoning, call a doctor at once or go to hospital, and seek help also for people who ate the same dish even if they have no symptoms yet. [T:SUS-145]
