# Paneer made by the NMSU acid-heat method

NMSU Extension groups paneer with queso fresco and queso blanco as cheeses made by curdling hot milk with an acid such as vinegar or lemon juice, without bacterial cultures or rennet. [T:MLE-054]

## Safety first

Make this cheese only from pasteurized milk; raw milk must be pasteurized before it is used for cheese. [T:MLE-015]

FDA recommends that people vulnerable to listeriosis avoid all queso fresco-type cheeses, even those made from pasteurized milk. [T:MLE-046] JarAtlas rule (cautious): because this paneer is made by the NMSU queso blanco method, JarAtlas applies the same advice to it. [T:MLE-046,MLE-054]

## What this card covers

The tested recipe NMSU gives for this group is no-rennet queso blanco, so this card uses that recipe for paneer. [T:MLE-054,MLE-078]

That recipe curdles the milk with 5% vinegar; it gives no amount of lemon juice, so do not replace the vinegar with lemon juice. [T:MLE-078]

## Ingredients

NMSU uses 1 gallon (3.76 l) of pasteurized milk, 4.6 fl oz (137 ml) of vinegar of 5% acidity, 9.2 fl oz (274 ml) of distilled water and 1 teaspoon (4.6 g) of salt. [T:MLE-078]

Spices can be added to taste. [T:MLE-078]

## Making

Heat the milk to 176 °F (80 °C) for 20 minutes. [T:MLE-053]

Mix the vinegar with the water and add it slowly to the hot milk until the whey is semi-clear and the curd particles start to stick together and become slightly stretchy. [T:MLE-079]

A piece of curd should stretch about 1 cm (0.39 inch) before it breaks; you may not need all of the vinegar. [T:MLE-079]

Pour the curd into a cloth bag and let the free whey run off. [T:MLE-079]

Work in the salt and spices, then press the curd; high pressure is not needed. [T:MLE-079]

## Storing

NMSU and UCCE seal this cheese in boilable bags and put it in boiling water for 5 minutes; packed this way it may keep for several weeks in the refrigerator, but it is best eaten fresh. [T:MLE-055]

Soft cheese with over 45% water keeps only 5–7 days in the refrigerator. [T:MLE-050]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): the source does not say how many weeks, so keep paneer in the refrigerator and eat it within 5 days, whether or not it was sealed and boiled. [T:MLE-050,MLE-055]

Listeria can grow at low temperatures, including in the refrigerator. [T:MLE-045]

## Who should be careful

In the USA the risk of listeriosis for pregnant women is about 10 times higher than for the general population. [T:MLE-047]

Where possible, cook queso fresco-type cheese in a dish to an internal temperature of at least 165 °F (74 °C). [T:MLE-048]
