# Plum fruit leather (NCHFP)

This card describes fruit leather from plums using the general NCHFP recipe: a thin layer of purée is dried at 140 °F (60 °C) in a dehydrator or oven and rolled up. [T:SUS-027,SUS-214]

## Purée

NCHFP does not describe tklapi: this card applies the general NCHFP recipe for fruit leather from fresh fruit to plums, not the traditional way of making tklapi. [T:SUS-210]

Select ripe or slightly overripe fruit; wash it in cool water, remove the peel, seeds and stem and cut it into chunks. [T:SUS-210]

Discard any fruit that shows decay, bruises or mould: such defects can affect all of the dried product. [T:SUS-223]

Purée the fruit until smooth; 2 cups of fruit make a 13 × 15-inch (about 33 × 38 cm) leather. [T:SUS-210]

If the fruit is light-coloured, add 2 teaspoons of lemon juice or 1/8 teaspoon (375 mg) of ascorbic acid for every 2 cups of fruit to prevent darkening. [T:SUS-026]

Sweetening is optional: for each 2 cups of fruit add 1/4–1/2 cup of sugar, corn syrup or honey. [T:SUS-211]

For longer storage, corn syrup or honey is best because it prevents crystals; sugar is fine for immediate use or short storage. [T:SUS-211]

## Trays

In an oven, use a 13 × 15-inch (about 33 × 38 cm) cookie pan with edges lined with plastic wrap; smooth out the wrinkles. [T:SUS-212]

Do not use waxed paper or aluminium foil. [T:SUS-212]

In a dehydrator, use specially designed plastic sheets or line plastic trays with plastic wrap. [T:SUS-212]

Do not use trays or screens made of galvanized mesh; also avoid copper and aluminium mesh. [T:SUS-008]

## Drying

Spread the purée evenly about 1/8 inch (about 3 mm) thick, not too close to the edge of the pan. [T:SUS-027,SUS-213]

Dry the leather at 140 °F (60 °C). [T:SUS-027]

Only use an oven if it can be set as low as 140 °F (60 °C); if it cannot go that low, the fruit cooks instead of drying. [T:SUS-069]

Check the oven temperature: place a calibrated oven thermometer on the rack or tray and check it every 2 hours throughout drying. [T:SUS-070]

Leave the oven door open 2–3 inches (about 5–7.5 cm) so moisture can escape. [T:SUS-071]

Larger leathers take longer to dry; approximate drying times are 6–8 hours in a dehydrator and up to 18 hours in an oven. [T:SUS-213]

The leather dries from the outside edge toward the centre; it is dry when touching the centre leaves no indentation. [T:SUS-214,SUS-027]

While still warm, peel the leather from the plastic and roll it up, let it cool and rewrap the roll in plastic. [T:SUS-214]

## Packing and storing

Pack only completely cooled rolls: warm dried food sweats, and that moisture can be enough for mould to grow. [T:SUS-024]

Store the dried fruit in clean, dry canning jars, plastic freezer containers with tight-fitting lids or plastic freezer bags. [T:SUS-064]

Packaging must be food grade: do not use trash bags or plastic or fibreboard containers that previously held non-food items. [T:SUS-065]

Pack amounts that can be used at one time: each opening exposes the dried fruit to air and moisture, which lowers quality and can lead to spoilage. [T:SUS-067]

Vacuum packaging is not a preservation method by itself: removing oxygen increases the risk of botulism, and a product that needs refrigeration still needs it. [T:SUS-025]

Store the dried fruit in a cool, dry, dark place. [T:SUS-066]

Fruit leather keeps up to 1 month at room temperature and up to 1 year in the freezer, tightly wrapped. [T:SUS-028]

This card does not cover fillings; if you spread the dried leather with, for example, cream cheese, cheese spread, peanut butter, chocolate or jam and roll it, store such rolls in the refrigerator. [T:SUS-222]

## When to throw away

Check the dried fruit often (glass shows condensation); if it has taken up moisture but is not spoiled, use it at once or dry it again and repack it. [T:SUS-023]

Throw away mouldy dried fruit; also throw away dried fruit that smells off. [T:SUS-022]
