# Honey-fermented garlic: why there is no recipe here

There is no recipe here for garlic fermented in raw honey. [T:-]

## !

- According to Ask Extension, there are no research-based recipes that prove fermenting garlic in raw honey is safe, or that show how to do it safely. [T:FER-070]
- Honey should not be fed to infants under 12 months of age: it is the one dietary source of Clostridium botulinum spores linked to infant botulism by both laboratory and epidemiologic studies. [T:SLO-036]
- K-State says fermenting garlic in raw honey is risky: garlic is a low-acid food, honey varies from low to high acidity, the water activity of the mixture is unknown, and botulism has occurred in garlic in oil. [T:SLO-037,FER-069]

## Why

According to an Extension expert, fermentation is not an approved, safe method of preserving garlic, and there are currently no validated recipes for fermenting garlic. [T:FER-068]

The FDA list gives garlic a pH of about 5.80. [T:ACI-106]

Honey can also be a source of infant botulism in children under age one. [T:FER-069]

JarAtlas has found no tested recipe for garlic fermented in honey from a checked source. [T:-]
