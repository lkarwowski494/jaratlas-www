# Quince paste (membrillo) for the refrigerator or freezer

This card follows the Food Preserving recipe for quince paste (dulce de membrillo): quince purée cooked with sugar for about 3 hours until thick, set in the refrigerator, then sliced and wrapped. [T:ACI-759]

## !

- The source allows up to 3 months in the refrigerator, but this is not a tested recipe: University of Wisconsin Extension says untested recipes can be prepared and stored in the refrigerator for up to 2 weeks, and not on the counter. [T:ACI-759,ACI-508]

## What this card covers

It covers paste kept in the refrigerator or freezer, not paste canned in jars. [T:-]

The US FDA list gives fresh or stewed quince an approximate pH of 3.12–3.40. [T:ACI-128]

## Ingredients

2.5 kg of quinces, 2 1/2 cups of water and 1/3 cup of lemon juice. [T:ACI-759]

About 1 kg of sugar: 3/4 to 1 cup of sugar for each cup of purée. [T:ACI-759]

The recipe uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

## Preparing

Cook and purée the quinces as the source directs; this card gives only the quantities, cooking time and storage checked against it. [T:ACI-759]

Measure the purée, add the sugar and cook for about 3 hours until thick. [T:ACI-759]

Pour the paste into a dish and let it set in the refrigerator for 12–24 hours. [T:ACI-759]

Slice the paste and wrap it in plastic wrap, or store it in airtight containers. [T:ACI-759]

## Storing

Keep the paste in the refrigerator for up to 2 weeks and do not store it on the counter. [T:ACI-508]

According to the source, the paste can also be frozen for up to 12 months. [T:ACI-759]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): keep the paste on the middle shelf of the refrigerator, not in the door: Wisconsin Extension assumes 40 °F (4.4 °C) or colder for refrigerator pickles, while a Polish sanitary inspection office gives about 10 °C for the door, even though it suggests the door for jars of preserves. [T:ACI-523]
