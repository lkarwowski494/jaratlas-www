# Thick plain yogurt (Illinois Extension recipe)

This card follows the University of Illinois Extension basic yogurt recipe: the milk is heated to 180–185 °F (82–85 °C) and the yogurt is incubated at 108–115 °F (42–46 °C). [T:MLE-024,MLE-026]

## !

- FDA lists yogurt made from unpasteurized milk among high-risk foods. [T:MLE-004]
- Once the yogurt has set, keep it in the refrigerator at 40 °F (4.4 °C) or lower. [T:MLE-027]

## Ingredients

Illinois Extension uses 1 quart of 1% low-fat milk and 2 tablespoons of plain non-fat yogurt as the starter. [T:MLE-072]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): make this yogurt only from pasteurized milk. [T:MLE-004]

For a thicker yogurt from nonfat skim milk, stir 1/4 cup of nonfat milk powder into the cold milk before you start heating it. [T:MLE-072]

The starter must contain live bacteria, because cultures with dead bacteria will not make yogurt. [T:MLE-073]

If you use purchased yogurt as the starter, use it before its “best by” or “sell by” date. [T:MLE-073]

If you use yogurt from your own earlier batch, use it within 5–7 days of making it. [T:MLE-029]

If you use a purchased bacterial culture, follow the manufacturer’s directions. [T:MLE-073]

## Making

Heat the milk slowly to 180–185 °F (82–85 °C). [T:MLE-024]

Stir it occasionally so it does not burn on the bottom, and check the temperature with a thermometer. [T:MLE-074]

Take it off the heat and let it cool to 108–115 °F (42–46 °C). [T:MLE-025]

Stir the starter into 1 cup of the warm milk until smooth, then pour it back into the pot and stir. [T:MLE-072]

Pour the milk into glass, food-safe plastic or metal containers and cover them with lids. [T:MLE-074]

Incubate at 108–115 °F (42–46 °C) for 6–10 hours without moving the containers. [T:MLE-026]

If you use a yogurt maker or another appliance, follow the manufacturer’s instructions. [T:MLE-074]

## Storing

As soon as the yogurt has set, move it to the refrigerator at 40 °F (4.4 °C) or lower. [T:MLE-027]

Cold stops the bacteria from making more lactic acid, which would make the yogurt more and more sour. [T:MLE-077]

Eat the yogurt within 14 days. [T:MLE-028]

## When it goes wrong

Illinois Extension says yogurt does not set when the starter had no live cultures, the incubation was too hot or too cold, or the equipment was not clean. [T:MLE-075]

It separates when it was incubated too long, was not refrigerated soon after incubation, or the milk was not heated enough before the starter went in. [T:MLE-075]

Off-flavours come from milk or starter that was not fresh, or from equipment that was not clean. [T:MLE-075]

Fermented dairy products must reach a pH of 4.6 or less within 48 hours to be considered pathogen-free. [T:MLE-021]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): if the yogurt has not set by the end of the incubation time, do not eat it. [T:MLE-021,MLE-026]
