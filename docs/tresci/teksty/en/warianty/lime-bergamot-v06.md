# Canned lime curd (NCHFP)

This card describes the lime curd variation of the NCHFP recipe for canned lemon curd: a cream of eggs, butter, sugar and bottled lime juice, cooked to a set temperature and processed in half-pint jars in a boiling-water bath. [T:SLO-038,SLO-236,SLO-043]

## Ingredients

For lime curd NCHFP uses the same recipe as for its canned lemon curd, with 1 cup of bottled lime juice and 1/4 cup of fresh lime zest instead of the lemon juice and zest. [T:SLO-043,SLO-236]

The other ingredients are 2 1/2 cups of superfine sugar, 3/4 cup of chilled unsalted butter cut into pieces of about 3/4 inch (about 1.9 cm), 7 large egg yolks and 4 large whole eggs; the lemon curd recipe makes about 3 to 4 half-pint jars. [T:SLO-236]

Use bottled lime juice, as the variation says: NCHFP uses bottled juice to standardize the acidity, because the acidity of fresh juice can vary. [T:SLO-043,SLO-039]

JarAtlas scope: this card covers lime curd only; NCHFP does not recommend other citrus curds, such as bergamot curd, for canning at this time. [T:SLO-043]

If you have no superfine sugar, grind granulated sugar in a grinder or food processor for 1 minute, let it settle and use it instead; do not use powdered sugar. [T:SLO-236]

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

Lime curd is the only variation NCHFP gives for its canned lemon curd recipe. [T:SLO-043]

## Equipment

The NCHFP recipe calls for a zester, a balloon whisk, a double boiler of at least 1 1/2 quarts (about 1.4 l), a strainer, a kitchen thermometer that reads up to at least 180 °F (about 82 °C), a glass or stainless steel bowl, a silicone spatula or spoon and equipment for boiling-water canning. [T:SLO-236]

## Preparing the jars and the canner

Wash 4 half-pint jars in warm, soapy water, rinse them well and keep them hot until filling; prepare the lids according to the manufacturer's directions. [T:SLO-237]

Fill the canner with enough water to cover the filled jars by 1 to 2 inches (about 2.5 to 5 cm). [T:SLO-237]

Using a thermometer, heat this water to 180 °F (about 82 °C) by the time the filled jars are ready. [T:SLO-237]

Do not heat the water in the canner above 180 °F (about 82 °C) before the jars go in: if it is too hot, the processing time will be too short. [T:SLO-040]

## Making the curd

Mix the sugar and zest in a small bowl and set aside for about 30 minutes; measure the juice and prepare the chilled butter. [T:SLO-238]

Heat water in the bottom pan of the double boiler until it boils gently; it should not boil vigorously or touch the bottom of the top pan. [T:SLO-238]

In the top pan, off the heat, whisk the egg yolks and whole eggs together, slowly whisk in the sugar and zest, then blend in the juice and add the butter pieces. [T:SLO-238]

Set the top pan over the boiling water and stir gently but constantly until the mixture reaches 170 °F (about 77 °C), checking with a food thermometer. [T:SLO-238]

Remove from the heat and keep stirring gently until the curd thickens, about 5 minutes. [T:SLO-238]

Strain the curd through a mesh strainer into a glass or stainless steel bowl and discard the zest left in the strainer. [T:SLO-238]

## Canning

Fill the hot strained curd into the clean, hot half-pint jars, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:SLO-239]

Remove air bubbles, adjust the headspace if needed, wipe the rims with a damp clean paper towel and put on two-piece metal lids. [T:SLO-239]

After the jars go in, the canner usually takes 25 to 30 minutes to reach a boil; start timing only when the water comes to a full boil over the tops of the jars. [T:SLO-237]

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process half-pint jars for 15 minutes. [T:SLO-038,SLO-239,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–6000 ft (above 300 m up to 1800 m) process them for 20 minutes. [T:SLO-038,SLO-239,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 25 minutes. [T:SLO-038,SLO-239,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

The NCHFP table gives a time only for half-pint jars. [T:SLO-038,SLO-241]

JarAtlas rule (cautious, NCHFP does not test metric jars): a half-pint (1 cup) holds about 236.6 ml, so a 0.25 l or 0.3 l jar has no tested time; use jars of up to 236 ml (rounded down), such as 0.2 l. [T:SLO-241]

## Cooling and storing

When you take the hot jars out of the canner, do not retighten the lids or bands; this can cut through the gasket and cause seal failures. [T:SLO-210]

Cool the jars at room temperature for 12 to 24 hours, then remove only the screw bands (the metal rings of two-piece lids), leaving the flat lids on the jars, and test the seal: press the middle of the lid, and if it springs up when you release it, the jar is not sealed. [T:SLO-196,SLO-212]

NCHFP calls Mason-type canning jars with self-sealing two-piece lids (a flat lid and a screw band) the best choice, and this seal test and the method on this card assume them; NCHFP does not recommend other commercial jars whose mouths cannot be sealed with two-piece canning lids for canning any food at home. [T:SLO-223,SLO-212]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): use this card only with jars that take two-piece canning lids; it gives no method for twist-off jars or jars with glass lids, so do not use them for this recipe. [T:SLO-223]

A sealed lid is concave, curved down slightly in the centre; if the centre is flat or bulging, the jar may not be sealed. [T:SLO-196]

If a jar has not sealed at this check, keep it in the refrigerator and eat it within several days. [T:SLO-197]

For best quality, store the jars in a cool, dark place, away from light. [T:SLO-239]

Store sealed jars in a clean, cool, dark, dry place, not above 95 °F (35 °C) and not near hot pipes, a stove or a furnace, in an uninsulated attic or in direct sunlight; in such conditions the food may spoil. [T:SLO-198]

Plan to use canned lime curd within 3 to 4 months. [T:SLO-041]

Instead of canning, you can freeze the curd: after straining, cool it to room temperature and pack it in freezer containers; it keeps for up to 1 year without quality changes. [T:SLO-240,SLO-042]

Thaw it in the refrigerator at 40 °F (about 4 °C) or lower for 24 hours before use, and eat it within 4 weeks of thawing. [T:SLO-240,SLO-042]

## When to throw it out

Browning or separation may appear with longer storage; throw the curd out whenever you see these changes. [T:SLO-041]

Before using a stored jar, check that the lid is tight and holds a vacuum: a good seal has a concave centre. [T:SLO-191]

Look for streaks of dried food running from the top of the jar, rising air bubbles and unnatural colour. [T:SLO-191]

When opening the jar, check for an unnatural smell, spurting liquid and cotton-like mould on the food or under the lid. [T:SLO-191]

Do not taste food from a jar with an unsealed lid or with signs of spoilage. [T:SLO-190]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): throw out such a jar. [T:SLO-190]
