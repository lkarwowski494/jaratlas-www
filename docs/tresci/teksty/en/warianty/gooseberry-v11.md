# Whole gooseberries in heavy syrup (NCHFP)

This card describes the NCHFP method for canning whole gooseberries, packed raw in a heavy syrup, in a boiling-water bath. [T:ACI-244,ACI-249,SLO-200]

## Preparing

Use these NCHFP directions only for the berries on its list, which includes gooseberries. [T:ACI-244]

Choose ripe, sweet berries with uniform color. [T:ACI-245]

NCHFP uses on average 8 pounds (about 3.6 kg) of berries for a load of 9 pint jars and 12 pounds (about 5.4 kg) for 7 quart jars. [T:ACI-246]

Wash 1 or 2 quarts (about 0.95–1.9 l) of berries at a time and drain them. [T:ACI-247]

Snip off the heads and tails of the gooseberries with scissors. [T:ACI-247]

NCHFP allows syrup, juice or water as the liquid; this card uses a heavy syrup, which the NCHFP syrup table lists as commonly used for gooseberries. [T:ACI-249,SLO-200]

Syrup helps keep the flavor, color and shape of the fruit, but it does not prevent spoilage. [T:ACI-067]

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

For a heavy syrup (about 40% sugar) NCHFP uses 5 cups of water and 3 1/4 cups of sugar for a load of 9 pint jars, or 7 3/4 cups of water and 5 1/4 cups of sugar for 7 quart jars; heat the water and sugar together to a boil. [T:SLO-200]

## Packing

NCHFP also gives a hot pack for gooseberries; this card describes only the raw pack. [T:ACI-248]

Pour 1/2 cup of the hot syrup into each clean jar. [T:ACI-247]

Fill the jars with the raw berries, shaking them down gently as you fill. [T:ACI-249]

Cover the berries with hot syrup, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:ACI-249]

Put on the lids and process. [T:ACI-249]

## Processing

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process pints for 15 minutes and quarts for 20. [T:ACI-069,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–3000 ft (above 300 m up to 900 m) process pints for 20 minutes and quarts for 25. [T:ACI-250,ACI-176]

At 3001–6000 ft (above 900 m up to 1800 m) process pints for 20 minutes and quarts for 30. [T:ACI-251,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process pints for 25 minutes and quarts for 35. [T:ACI-252,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 3000 ft becomes 900 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

NCHFP times are for US jar sizes: a pint holds about 473 ml and a quart about 946 ml. [T:ACI-174]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): for a jar of up to 473 ml use the pint time, and for a larger jar of up to 946 ml, for example 0.5 l or 0.9 l, use the quart time. [T:ACI-175]

This NCHFP recipe gives no time for jars larger than a quart, such as 1 l, so JarAtlas recommends smaller jars. [T:ACI-175]

## Cooling and storing

When you take the hot jars out of the canner, do not retighten the lids or bands; this can cut through the gasket and cause seal failures. [T:SLO-210]

In raw-packed jars the food and liquid level will be noticeably lower after cooling. [T:SLO-210]

If any jar loses a lot of liquid during processing, do not open it to add more. [T:SLO-210]

Cool the jars at room temperature for 12 to 24 hours, then remove the screw bands and test the seal: press the middle of the lid, and if it springs up when you release it, the jar is not sealed. [T:SLO-196,SLO-212]

A sealed lid is concave, curved down slightly in the centre; if the centre is flat or bulging, the jar may not be sealed. [T:SLO-196]

If a jar has not sealed at this check, keep it in the refrigerator and eat it within several days. [T:SLO-197]

Store sealed jars in a clean, cool, dark, dry place, not above 95 °F (35 °C) and not near hot pipes, a stove or a furnace, in an uninsulated attic or in direct sunlight; in such conditions the food may spoil. [T:SLO-198]

## When to throw it out

Before using a stored jar, check that the lid is tight and holds a vacuum: a good seal has a concave centre. [T:SLO-191]

Look for streaks of dried food running from the top of the jar, rising air bubbles and unnatural colour. [T:SLO-191]

When opening the jar, check for an unnatural smell, spurting liquid and cotton-like mould on the food or under the lid. [T:SLO-191]

Do not taste food from a jar with an unsealed lid or with signs of spoilage. [T:SLO-190]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): throw out such a jar. [T:SLO-190]
