# Applesauce (NCHFP)

This card describes NCHFP applesauce, hot packed and processed in a boiling-water bath. [T:SLO-092]

## Choosing apples

NCHFP keeps its apple recipes but advises using fresh, sound apples, and if you are unsure of their pH, refrigerate or freeze apple products instead of storing them at room temperature. [T:SLO-048]

## Making the sauce

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

Wash, peel and core the apples. [T:SLO-121]

If you like, slice them into water containing ascorbic acid to prevent browning. [T:SLO-121]

Put the drained slices in a large 8- to 10-quart pot (about 7.5–9.5 l) and add 1/2 cup of water. [T:SLO-121]

Heat quickly until tender, stirring occasionally to prevent burning; this takes 5–20 minutes, depending on the maturity and variety of the apples. [T:SLO-121]

Press through a sieve or food mill, or skip this step if you prefer chunky sauce. [T:SLO-121]

The sauce may be packed without sugar, or you can add 1/8 cup of sugar per quart of sauce and more to taste. [T:SLO-122]

## Filling the jars

Reheat the sauce to boiling and fill the jars with hot sauce, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:SLO-122]

Put on the lids and process. [T:SLO-122]

## Processing

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process pints in a boiling-water bath for 15 minutes and quarts for 20. [T:SLO-123,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–3000 ft (above 300 m up to 900 m) process pints for 20 minutes and quarts for 25. [T:SLO-124,ACI-176]

At 3001–6000 ft (above 900 m up to 1800 m) process pints for 20 minutes and quarts for 30. [T:SLO-125,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process pints for 25 minutes and quarts for 35. [T:SLO-126,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 3000 ft becomes 900 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

NCHFP times are for US jar sizes: a pint holds about 473 ml and a quart about 946 ml. [T:ACI-174]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): for a jar of up to 473 ml use the pint time, and for a larger jar of up to 946 ml, for example 0.5 l or 0.9 l, use the quart time. [T:ACI-175]

This NCHFP recipe gives no time for jars larger than a quart, such as 1 l, so use smaller jars. [T:ACI-175]
