# Dried grapefruit slices (UC ANR)

This card describes drying evenly cut grapefruit slices in a dehydrator or oven at 140 °F (60 °C), following a UC ANR handout for citrus. [T:SUS-331,SUS-001]

## Choosing and preparing

The UC ANR handout covers citrus in general; this card applies it to grapefruit. [T:SUS-331]

Wash and sort the fruit, and discard any that shows decay, bruises or mould: such defects can affect all of the dried product. [T:SUS-223]

Cut the fruit evenly into slices 1/8–1/4 inch (about 3–6 mm) thick. [T:SUS-331]

## Drying

Dry the slices at 140 °F (60 °C) in a dehydrator or oven. [T:SUS-001]

The UC ANR handout sets the dehydrator to 130–135 °F (about 54–57 °C) and dries the slices for 12–18 hours, flipping them halfway; this card uses the higher temperature of 140 °F (60 °C). [T:SUS-331,SUS-001]

Dry until the slices are brittle with a leathery texture. [T:SUS-331]

Do not use trays or screens made of galvanized mesh; also avoid copper and aluminium mesh. [T:SUS-008]

Only use an oven if it can be set as low as 140 °F (60 °C); if it cannot go that low, the fruit cooks instead of drying. [T:SUS-069]

In an oven, keep 140–150 °F (about 60–66 °C): place a calibrated oven thermometer on the rack or tray and check it every 2 hours throughout drying. [T:SUS-070]

Leave the oven door open 2–3 inches (about 5–7.5 cm) so moisture can escape. [T:SUS-071]

In a conventional oven, drying can take up to twice as long as in a dehydrator, depending on air circulation. [T:SUS-072]

Fruit scorches easily near the end of drying: when drying is almost done, turn the oven heat off and open the door wide for one more hour. [T:SUS-073]

## Cooling and conditioning

Before checking for doneness, take out a few slices and let them cool to room temperature: warm fruit seems softer, moister and more pliable than it really is. [T:SUS-062]

After drying, cool the fruit for 30–60 minutes before packing; do not wait too long to pack it, because moisture could get back into it. [T:SUS-068]

Pack only completely cooled fruit: warm dried food sweats, and that moisture can be enough for mould to grow. [T:SUS-024]

Condition the slices for 7 days: put them in a container and shake it daily to spread the moisture evenly. [T:SUS-331]

If condensation develops in the container, return the slices to the dehydrator. [T:SUS-019]

## Packing and storing

Store the slices in an airtight container with a food-safe desiccant packet. [T:SUS-331]

Store the dried fruit in clean, dry canning jars, plastic freezer containers with tight-fitting lids or plastic freezer bags. [T:SUS-064]

Packaging must be food grade: do not use trash bags or plastic or fibreboard containers that previously held non-food items. [T:SUS-065]

Pack amounts that can be used at one time: each opening exposes the dried fruit to air and moisture, which lowers quality and can lead to spoilage. [T:SUS-067]

Vacuum packaging is not a preservation method by itself: removing oxygen increases the risk of botulism, and a product that needs refrigeration still needs it. [T:SUS-025]

Store the dried fruit in a cool, dry, dark place. [T:SUS-066]

The UC ANR handout gives storage of up to one year. [T:SUS-331]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): NCHFP gives most dried fruits 1 year at 60 °F (about 16 °C) but only 6 months at 80 °F (about 27 °C), so in a warm room use the slices within 6 months. [T:SUS-020]

## When to throw away

Check the dried fruit often (glass shows condensation); if it has taken up moisture but is not spoiled, use it at once or dry it again and repack it. [T:SUS-023]

Throw away mouldy dried fruit; also throw away dried fruit that smells off. [T:SUS-022]
