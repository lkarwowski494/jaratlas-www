# Seedless raspberry jam without added pectin (NCHFP recipe)

This card describes the NCHFP recipe for berry jam without added pectin, in its seedless version, made with raspberries. [T:SLO-077,SLO-149,SLO-146]

## Ingredients

NCHFP uses 9 cups of crushed berries and 6 cups of sugar; the basic recipe, with seeds, makes about 7 or 8 half-pint jars. [T:SLO-145,SLO-156]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): measure the 9 cups of crushed berries before heating and sieving them; measuring 9 cups of already sieved pulp would give less sugar for the fruit than the recipe. [T:SLO-156,SLO-149]

For NCHFP jams without added pectin, berries include raspberries, as well as blackberries, gooseberries and strawberries. [T:SLO-146]

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

NCHFP warns not to reduce the sugar in traditional recipes: too little sugar prevents gelling and may allow yeasts and molds to grow. [T:SLO-001]

## Preparing

Sterilize the jars. [T:SLO-147]

NCHFP puts the empty jars right side up on the rack in a boiling-water canner and fills the canner and jars with hot (not boiling) water to 1 inch (about 2.5 cm) above the tops of the jars. [T:SLO-120]

To sterilize empty jars, NCHFP boils them for 10 minutes below 1000 ft, and above that 1 minute longer for each additional thousand feet. [T:SLO-118]

JarAtlas rule for metres: boil the jars for 10 minutes up to 300 m, and add 1 minute for every further 300 m or part of it. [T:SLO-119]

Wash the raspberries before crushing them. [T:SLO-147]

For seedless jam, heat the crushed berries until soft and press them through a sieve or food mill. [T:SLO-149]

## Making the jam

Then add the sugar to the sieved fruit. [T:SLO-149,SLO-147]

Bring slowly to a boil, stirring occasionally until the sugar dissolves. [T:SLO-147]

Once the sugar has dissolved, cook rapidly to, or almost to, the jellying point, depending on whether you want a firm or a soft jam. [T:SLO-147]

As the mixture thickens, stir often to keep it from sticking. [T:SLO-147]

## How to tell it is ready

Temperature test: at sea level the jam is ready at 220 °F (about 104 °C), 8 °F (about 4.4 °C) above the boiling point of water. [T:SLO-154]

Higher up, use the NCHFP table for jams: 218 °F at 1000 ft, 216 °F at 2000 ft, 214 °F at 3000 ft, 212 °F at 4000 ft, 211 °F at 5000 ft, 209 °F at 6000 ft, 207 °F at 7000 ft and 205 °F at 8000 ft (1000 ft is about 300 m). [T:SLO-154]

Freezer test: take the jam off the heat, put a little boiling jam on a cold plate and place it in the freezer compartment of the refrigerator for a few minutes; if it gels, the jam is ready to fill. [T:SLO-155]

Jam keeps thickening as it cools. [T:SLO-154]

## Canning

Just before filling, remove and drain the hot sterilized jars one at a time. [T:SLO-120]

Remove the jam from the heat and fill it hot into hot, sterile jars, leaving 1/4 inch (about 0.6 cm) of headspace. [T:SLO-148]

Wipe the jar rims with a damp clean paper towel, put on two-piece metal lids and process in a boiling-water canner. [T:SLO-148]

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process half-pint and pint jars for 5 minutes. [T:SLO-150,ACI-176]

At 1001–6000 ft (above 300 m up to 1800 m) process them for 10 minutes. [T:SLO-151,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 15 minutes. [T:SLO-152,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

The NCHFP table gives times only for half-pint and pint jars. [T:SLO-153]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): a pint holds about 473 ml, so a 0.5 l jar has no tested time in this recipe; use jars of up to 473 ml. [T:ACI-179,ACI-174]

## Storing

Keep opened jam in the refrigerator at 40 °F (about 4 °C) or lower. [T:SLO-018]

Regular full-sugar cooked jams like this one are best kept in the refrigerator for 1 month after opening. [T:SLO-018]

## When to throw it out

Discard jam with mold on it; USDA and microbiologists advise against scooping out the mold and eating the rest. [T:SLO-012]
