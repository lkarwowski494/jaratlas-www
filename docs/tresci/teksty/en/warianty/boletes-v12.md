# Dried bolete powder (CSU)

This card describes how to dry boletes reliably identified as edible in a dehydrator or oven at 140 °F (60 °C), without blanching, and grind them into a powder. [T:SUS-001,SUS-036,SUS-138,SUS-040]

## !

- Drying and cooking do not destroy the toxins of poisonous mushrooms, and only an expert can tell edible species from poisonous ones. [T:SUS-035]
- Dry only cultivated or wild mushrooms that are safe to eat; unless you are an expert in mushroom identification, preserve only commercially sold mushrooms. [T:SUS-138]
- In Poland: if in doubt whether mushrooms are edible, take them fresh, before drying, to a sanitary-epidemiological station (sanepid); mushroom experts or classifiers there assess free of charge whether they are edible, inedible or poisonous. [T:SUS-142]

## Identification before drying

Outside Poland this card does not name an institution that assesses mushrooms: if you are not sure a mushroom is edible, do not dry or eat it. [T:SUS-035,SUS-138]

Take whole fruiting bodies, with cap and stem, for assessment, because important identifying features are also on the stem; so have the mushrooms assessed before slicing and drying. [T:SUS-143]

In Poland, a mushroom expert (grzyboznawca) is authorised to assess whether fresh and dried mushrooms are fit to eat. [T:SUS-144]

## Choosing and preparing

Scrub the mushrooms. [T:SUS-036]

Discard tough, woody stalks and slice tender stalks 1/4 inch thick. [T:SUS-036]

Peel large mushrooms and slice them; leave small mushrooms whole. [T:SUS-036]

Dip the mushrooms for 10 minutes in a solution of 1 teaspoon citric acid per 1 quart of water, then drain. [T:SUS-036]

Do not blanch the mushrooms. [T:SUS-036]

## Drying

Arrange the prepared mushrooms on trays in a single or thin layer, no more than 1/2 inch (about 13 mm) deep. [T:SUS-107]

Dry the mushrooms at 140 °F (60 °C) in a dehydrator or oven. [T:SUS-001]

Do not dry mushrooms outdoors: dry them under the controlled conditions of an oven or dehydrator. [T:SUS-003]

Do not use trays or screens made of galvanized mesh; also avoid copper and aluminium mesh. [T:SUS-008]

Only use an oven if it can be set as low as 140 °F (60 °C); if it cannot go that low, the mushrooms cook instead of drying. [T:SUS-069]

In an oven, keep 140–150 °F (about 60–66 °C): place a calibrated oven thermometer on the rack or tray and check it every 2 hours throughout drying. [T:SUS-070]

Leave the oven door open 2–3 inches (about 5–7.5 cm) so moisture can escape. [T:SUS-071]

In a conventional oven, drying can take up to twice as long as in a dehydrator, depending on air circulation. [T:SUS-072]

Dry the mushrooms for 8–12 hours, until dry and leathery. [T:SUS-036]

When drying is complete, some pieces are moister than others because of their size and location during drying; conditioning distributes residual moisture evenly and lowers the chance of spoilage, so condition these dried pieces. [T:SUS-203]

Put the cooled dried pieces in large plastic or glass containers about two-thirds full, cover lightly and keep them in a warm, dry, well-ventilated place for 4–10 days, stirring or shaking daily. [T:SUS-203]

If beads of moisture form inside, return the food to the dehydrator, then condition it again. [T:SUS-203]

## Using

Make the powder by grinding the dried mushrooms in a food mill, food processor or blender. [T:SUS-040]

The Polish Minister of Health reminds people to eat only edible mushrooms that have been heat-treated, such as fried or cooked. [T:SUS-146]

So add the powder to dishes you cook or fry, not raw. [T:SUS-146]

## Packing and storing

Pack only completely cooled mushrooms: warm dried food sweats, and that moisture can be enough for mould to grow. [T:SUS-024]

Store the dried mushrooms in clean, dry canning jars, plastic freezer containers with tight-fitting lids or plastic freezer bags. [T:SUS-064]

Keep the powder in airtight containers: dry foods kept out of airtight containers may absorb moisture from the air, and powders may clump. [T:SUS-137]

Packaging must be food grade: do not use trash bags or plastic or fibreboard containers that previously held non-food items. [T:SUS-065]

Pack amounts that can be used at one time: each opening exposes the dried food to air and moisture, which lowers quality and can lead to spoilage. [T:SUS-067]

Vacuum packaging is not a preservation method by itself: removing oxygen increases the risk of botulism, and a product that needs refrigeration still needs it. [T:SUS-025]

Store the dried mushrooms in a cool, dry, dark place. [T:SUS-066]

## When to throw away

Check the dried vegetables often (glass shows condensation); if they have taken up moisture but are not spoiled, use them at once or dry them again and repack them. [T:SUS-023]

Throw away mouldy dried vegetables; also throw away any that smell off. [T:SUS-022]

## Symptoms of poisoning

With the most dangerous mushrooms, such as the death cap, the first symptoms often appear long after eating, sometimes only after several days, and after a temporary improvement the condition can worsen rapidly; with symptoms of poisoning, call a doctor at once or go to hospital, and seek help also for people who ate the same dish even if they have no symptoms yet. [T:SUS-145]
