# Green salsa with green tomatoes (NCHFP Tomatillo Green Salsa)

This card describes the NCHFP Tomatillo Green Salsa recipe made with green tomatoes instead of tomatillos, as the recipe allows: the vegetables are cooked with bottled lemon or lime juice, hot packed into pint jars and processed in a boiling-water bath. [T:ACI-430,ACI-427,SLO-704,SLO-705] NCHFP writes: “You may use green tomatoes in this recipe instead of tomatillos.” [T:ACI-430]

## Why you must not change the recipe

The FDA list gives tomatoes a pH of about 4.30–4.90, so some tomatoes have a pH slightly above 4.6. [T:ACI-093,ACI-008]

Tomatoes canned as an acid food must be acidified to pH 4.6 or lower. [T:ACI-008]

Green tomatoes are more acidic than ripe ones and are canned by the same recommendations, with added acid. [T:ACI-050]

Do not use green tomatoes from dead or frost-killed vines. [T:ACI-049]

You must add acid to canned salsas, because their natural acidity may not be high enough. [T:ACI-037]

This recipe gets its acid from 1 cup of bottled lemon or lime juice. [T:ACI-427]

For salsas NCHFP recommends only commercially bottled lemon juice, not fresh juice. [T:ACI-039]

The only other change you can safely make, besides using green tomatoes instead of tomatillos, is the amount of spices and herbs; do not alter the proportions of vegetables to acid and do not substitute vinegar for the lemon juice. [T:ACI-430]

Do not increase the total amount of peppers. [T:ACI-040]

Do not thicken the salsa with flour or cornstarch before canning. [T:ACI-042]

Do not can a salsa that does not follow a research-tested recipe; NCHFP says such salsa may be frozen or kept in the refrigerator, but this card gives no method or storage time for that. [T:ACI-041]

## Ingredients

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

For about 5 pint jars NCHFP lists 5 cups of chopped tomatillos, 1 1/2 cups of seeded, chopped long green chiles, 1/2 cup of seeded, finely chopped jalapeño peppers, 4 cups of chopped onions, 1 cup of bottled lemon or lime juice and 6 cloves of garlic, finely chopped. [T:ACI-427,SLO-703]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): NCHFP gives no separate amount or preparation for green tomatoes, and the proportions of vegetables to acid must not change, so wash and chop the green tomatoes and use the same 5 cups in place of the tomatillos. [T:SLO-708]

Seasonings: 1 tablespoon of salt and 1 teaspoon of black pepper, and optionally 1 tablespoon of ground cumin and 3 tablespoons of dried oregano leaves. [T:SLO-703]

If you make the salsa with tomatillos, remove their dry outer husks and wash them thoroughly; they do not need to be peeled or seeded. [T:SLO-704]

## Preparing the peppers

Wear plastic or rubber gloves and do not touch your face while handling or cutting hot peppers; if you do not wear gloves, wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water before touching your face or eyes. [T:ACI-267]

The jalapeño peppers do not need to be peeled. [T:SLO-697]

The skin of long green chiles may be tough and can be removed by heating the peppers. [T:SLO-697]

To peel the chiles, slit each pepper along the side to let steam escape. [T:SLO-697]

Blister the skins in a hot oven at 400 °F (about 204 °C) or under a broiler for 6 to 8 minutes, or for several minutes on a hot burner covered with heavy wire mesh. [T:SLO-697]

Then put the peppers in a pan, cover them with a damp cloth, let them cool for several minutes, slip off the skins, discard the seeds and chop the peppers. [T:SLO-697]

## Cooking and filling

Combine all the ingredients in a large saucepan and stir frequently over high heat until the mixture begins to boil. [T:SLO-704]

Reduce the heat and simmer for 20 minutes, stirring occasionally. [T:SLO-704]

Ladle the hot salsa into clean, hot pint jars, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:SLO-704]

Remove air bubbles, adjust the headspace if needed, wipe the jar rims with a dampened clean paper towel and put on two-piece metal canning lids. [T:SLO-704]

## Processing

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process pint jars in a boiling-water bath for 15 minutes. [T:SLO-705,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–6000 ft (above 300 m up to 1800 m) process them for 20 minutes. [T:SLO-706,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 25 minutes. [T:SLO-707,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

NCHFP times are for US jar sizes: a pint holds about 473 ml and a quart about 946 ml. [T:ACI-174]

The NCHFP table gives times only for pint jars. [T:SLO-705]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): a pint holds about 473 ml, so a 0.5 l jar has no tested time in this recipe; use jars of up to 473 ml. [T:ACI-179,ACI-174]

## Cooling and storing

When you take the hot jars out of the canner, do not retighten the lids or bands; this can cut through the gasket and cause seal failures. [T:SLO-210]

Cool the jars at room temperature for 12 to 24 hours, then remove only the screw bands (the metal rings of two-piece lids), leaving the flat lids on the jars, and test the seal: press the middle of the lid, and if it springs up when you release it, the jar is not sealed. [T:SLO-196,SLO-212]

NCHFP calls Mason-type canning jars with self-sealing two-piece lids (a flat lid and a screw band) the best choice, and this seal test and the method on this card assume them; NCHFP does not recommend other commercial jars whose mouths cannot be sealed with two-piece canning lids for canning any food at home. [T:SLO-223,SLO-212]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): use this card only with jars that take two-piece canning lids; it gives no method for twist-off jars or jars with glass lids, so do not use them for this recipe. [T:SLO-223]

A sealed lid is concave, curved down slightly in the centre; if the centre is flat or bulging, the jar may not be sealed. [T:SLO-196]

If a jar has not sealed at this check, keep it in the refrigerator and eat it within several days. [T:SLO-197]

Store sealed jars in a clean, cool, dark, dry place, not above 95 °F (35 °C) and not near hot pipes, a stove or a furnace, in an uninsulated attic or in direct sunlight; in such conditions the food may spoil. [T:SLO-198]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): keep an opened jar in the refrigerator and use it within 1 week. [T:SLO-322]

## When to throw it out

Before using a stored jar, check that the lid is tight and holds a vacuum: a good seal has a concave centre. [T:SLO-191]

Look for streaks of dried food running from the top of the jar, rising air bubbles and unnatural colour. [T:SLO-191]

When opening the jar, check for an unnatural smell, spurting liquid and cotton-like mould on the food or under the lid. [T:SLO-191]

Do not taste food from a jar with an unsealed lid or with signs of spoilage. [T:SLO-190]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): throw out such a jar. [T:SLO-190]

NCHFP warns that spoiled low-acid foods, including tomatoes, may show different signs of spoilage or very few, so every suspect jar of such food should be treated as if it had produced botulinum toxin and handled carefully. [T:SLO-350]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): handle every suspect jar from this card this way, because its main ingredient is low in acid or close to the acidity limit. [T:SLO-350]

If a suspect jar is still sealed, put it in a heavy garbage bag, close the bag and put it in a regular trash container. [T:SLO-350]

If it is unsealed, open or leaking, NCHFP says it should be detoxified before disposal; JarAtlas does not repeat that procedure here, so follow the detoxification and clean-up steps on the NCHFP page “Identifying and Handling Spoiled Canned Food” (nchfp.uga.edu/how/can/general-information/identifying-and-handling-spoiled-canned-food/) and avoid contact with suspect food or liquid. [T:SLO-350]

Contact with botulinum toxin can be fatal if it is swallowed or enters through any openings in the skin, so wear rubber or heavy plastic gloves when handling suspect jars and cleaning up. [T:SLO-350]
