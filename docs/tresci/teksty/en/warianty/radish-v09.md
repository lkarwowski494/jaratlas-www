# Pickled radish seed pods for the refrigerator

This card follows the Redemption Permaculture recipe for pickled radish seed pods, checked against the Oregon State University Extension rules for quick pickles; JarAtlas keeps them up to 2 weeks in the refrigerator. [T:ACI-862,ACI-751,ACI-508]

## !

- Oregon State University Extension advises that quick pickles have at least as much vinegar as water, with vinegar of 5% acidity; this recipe uses equal parts, so do not add more water or less vinegar. [T:ACI-751,ACI-862,ACI-016]
- Use only vinegar labelled 5% acidity; white and cider vinegar can be used interchangeably; do not use wine vinegars, homemade vinegar or lemon juice. [T:ACI-509]
- The source says the pods keep up to a month in the refrigerator, but this is not a tested recipe: University of Wisconsin Extension says untested recipes can be prepared and stored in the refrigerator for up to 2 weeks, and not on the counter. [T:ACI-862,ACI-508]
- These pickles are not heat processed, so they must be kept refrigerated; do not decrease the amount of vinegar, because an unsafe product may result; for a milder taste add sugar rather than reduce the vinegar. [T:ACI-501]

## Ingredients

1 cup of radish seed pods, 1/2 cup of white vinegar and 1/2 cup of water. [T:ACI-862]

The recipe uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

The brine also has 1 tablespoon of salt, 1 tablespoon of sugar, a garlic clove and a bay leaf. [T:ACI-862]

Vinegar with less than 5% acidity should never be used in home food preservation. [T:ACI-516]

Do not change the proportions of vinegar, vegetables and water in the recipe. [T:ACI-016]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): do not replace it with a stronger vinegar and do not dilute a stronger vinegar with water yourself; Penn State Extension advises staying within 5–7% acidity for home food preservation and diluting vinegar only when a tested recipe says so. [T:ACI-209,ACI-210]

## Preparing

JarAtlas rule (cautious): use sterilized jars, as an Ask Extension expert advises for refrigerator pickles, and sterilize them as UGA directs for flavored vinegars: wash them in warm, soapy water, then immerse them completely in boiling water for 10 minutes. [T:ACI-751,NAP-070]

Bring the brine to a boil, add the pods and simmer them, covered, for about 10 minutes. [T:ACI-862]

Transfer them to the jar, making sure the pods are covered with brine. [T:ACI-862]

Let the jar cool, then refrigerate it straight away. [T:ACI-862,ACI-751]

## Storing

The pods are ready after 24 hours. [T:ACI-862]

Use them within 2 weeks and do not store them on the counter. [T:ACI-508]

These pickles are not heat processed, so they are not shelf-stable at room temperature and must be kept in the refrigerator. [T:ACI-501,ACI-022]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): keep the jar on the middle shelf of the refrigerator, not in the door: Wisconsin Extension assumes 40 °F (4.4 °C) or colder for refrigerator pickles, while a Polish sanitary inspection office gives about 10 °C for the door, even though it suggests the door for jars of preserves. [T:ACI-523]

## Why the vinegar matters

Fresh vegetables, except most tomatoes, are low-acid foods, with a pH above 4.6. [T:ACI-005]

The acid level must be adequate and even throughout the product to prevent the growth of botulism bacteria. [T:ACI-017]
