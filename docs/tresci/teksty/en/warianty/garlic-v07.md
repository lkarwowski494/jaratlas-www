# Garlic cloves in vinegar for the refrigerator (UC ANR method)

This card describes the UC ANR method for storing peeled garlic cloves in undiluted vinegar in the refrigerator. [T:ACI-512]

## Ingredients

Peeled garlic cloves and undiluted (full-strength) vinegar. [T:ACI-512]

For this card JarAtlas follows the Wisconsin Extension rule for quick pickles: use only vinegar labelled 5% acidity; white and cider vinegar are interchangeable; do not use wine vinegars, homemade vinegar or lemon juice. [T:ACI-509]

Vinegar with less than 5% acidity should never be used in home food preservation. [T:ACI-516]

A small amount of dried spices, such as peppercorns, hot chili flakes, cumin seeds or bay leaves, may be added. [T:ACI-711]

## Preparing

Submerge the peeled cloves in the vinegar and store the jar in the refrigerator. [T:ACI-512]

The vinegar gives an acidic environment, with a pH below 4.6, in which Clostridium botulinum cannot grow. [T:ACI-512]

The garlic-flavored vinegar and the cloves may be used to flavor dishes. [T:ACI-711]

## Storing

The garlic in vinegar should keep for about 4 months in the refrigerator; label the jar with the preparation date and a “best before” date. [T:ACI-513]

Longer storage should not make it unsafe, but mold may develop and the flavor may change. [T:ACI-513]

Do not store it at room temperature, because it rapidly develops mold. [T:ACI-435]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): keep the jar on the middle shelf of the refrigerator, not in the door: US extension guidance (for example Wisconsin Extension) assumes 40 °F (4.4 °C) or colder for refrigerator pickles, while a Polish sanitary inspection office gives about 10 °C for the door, even though it suggests the door for jars of preserves. [T:ACI-523]

## When to throw it out

If there are signs of mold or yeast growth on the surface of the vinegar, discard both the cloves and the liquid. [T:ACI-435]
