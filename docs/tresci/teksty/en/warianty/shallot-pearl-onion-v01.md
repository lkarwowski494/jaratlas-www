# Quick pickled onions for the refrigerator (UC Master Food Preservers)

This card describes the UC Master Food Preserver recipe for pickled onions; recipes called “quick pickled” are refrigerator pickles and are not shelf-stable at room temperature. [T:ACI-507,ACI-024]

## Ingredients

Per jar: 1 cup white vinegar (or cider vinegar), 1 cup water, 1 tablespoon canning or pickling salt and 2 teaspoons sugar; spices are optional. [T:ACI-507,ACI-707]

The recipe gives no amount of onions: use as many as fit tightly in the jar, because the brine ratios matter most. [T:ACI-707]

Use only vinegar labelled 5% acidity; white and cider vinegar can be used interchangeably; do not use wine vinegars, homemade vinegar or lemon juice. [T:ACI-509]

Vinegar with less than 5% acidity should never be used in home food preservation. [T:ACI-516]

Do not change the proportions of vinegar, vegetables and water in the recipe. [T:ACI-016]

Do not replace it with a stronger vinegar and do not dilute a stronger vinegar with water yourself; Penn State Extension advises staying within 5–7% acidity for home food preservation and diluting vinegar only when a tested recipe says so. [T:ACI-209,ACI-210]

## Preparing

Peel and rinse the onions, then slice them thinly. [T:ACI-707]

Pack the onions tightly into a jar or container with a lid. [T:ACI-707]

Boil the brine for 2 minutes and fill the jar, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace, then cover it. [T:ACI-707]

## Storing

Give the flavor a day or two to develop, and use the onions within 2 weeks. [T:ACI-507]

These pickles are not heat processed, so they are not shelf-stable at room temperature and must be kept in the refrigerator. [T:ACI-501,ACI-022]

Do not decrease the amount of vinegar, because an unsafe product may result; for a milder taste add sugar rather than reduce the vinegar. [T:ACI-501]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): keep the jar on the middle shelf of the refrigerator, not in the door: Wisconsin Extension assumes 40 °F (4.4 °C) or colder for refrigerator pickles, while a Polish sanitary inspection office gives about 10 °C for the door, even though it suggests the door for jars of preserves. [T:ACI-523]

## Why the vinegar matters

Fresh vegetables are low-acid foods, with a pH above 4.6. [T:ACI-005]

The acid level must be adequate and even throughout the product to prevent the growth of botulism bacteria. [T:ACI-017]
