# Rose petals in honey: why there is no recipe here

There is no recipe here for rose petals kept in honey. [T:-]

## !

- According to Oregon State University Extension, covering food with honey does not preserve it: lab trials found bacteria growing where the food meets the honey. [T:SLO-465]
- Oregon State University Extension limits high-moisture, low-acid ingredients, such as fresh garlic or herbs, to no more than 15% of a honey infusion by weight, using hot infusion only, with the honey kept above 140°F (60°C). [T:SLO-468] After the infusion, the hot honey is strained to remove all pieces, so this method does not keep rose petals in the honey. [T:SLO-821]
- Do not give honey or products made with honey to infants under 1 year of age. [T:SLO-469]
- The Polish National Centre for Nutrition Education (NCEŻ) lists honey among the foods an infant should avoid because of the risk of poisoning. [T:SLO-470]
- According to NDSU Extension, some flowers should not be eaten, so identify flowers correctly before eating them; flowers from a florist are meant to look at, not to eat, because some pesticides used on them have not been approved for edible plants. [T:SLO-489]

## Why

Herbs, flowers and infusions made from them are not naturally high-acid ingredients; to be canned, they need a tested recipe with an added amount of acid, sugar or both, even when the infusion is strained. [T:SLO-068]

Honey ranges in pH from 3.1 to 6.1, and some pathogens can grow at a pH as low as 3.7; honey is kept safe by its low water activity, not by its acidity. [T:SLO-466]

JarAtlas has found no tested recipe for rose petals in honey from a checked source. [T:-]
