# Canned apricots, halved or sliced (NCHFP)

This card describes the NCHFP method for canning halved or sliced apricots in a boiling-water bath. [T:SLO-097]

## !

- Apricot pH varies: the US FDA list gives 3.30–4.80, so the upper end is above 4.6, the limit for acid foods. [T:E3-002,ACI-001] NCHFP gives a tested process without added lemon juice or citric acid (the ascorbic acid solution only keeps the fruit from darkening), and JarAtlas knows no tested amount of acid for this recipe; follow the recipe exactly, or, for the more cautious option, freeze the apricots by the NCHFP method. [T:E3-002,ACI-218]

## Preparing

Choose firm, well-colored, mature apricots of a quality fit for eating fresh. [T:ACI-218]

If you do not remove the skins, wash the fruit well. [T:ACI-218]

Removing the skins is optional: dip the washed fruit in boiling water for 30–60 seconds until the skins loosen, dip it quickly in cold water and slip off the skins. [T:ACI-218]

Cut the apricots in half, remove the pits and slice them if you like. [T:ACI-218]

To prevent darkening, keep the peeled fruit in an ascorbic acid solution. [T:ACI-218]

Prepare and boil a very light, light or medium syrup, or pack the apricots in water, apple juice or white grape juice. [T:ACI-219]

Syrup helps keep the flavor, color and shape of the fruit, but it does not prevent spoilage. [T:ACI-067]

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

For a very light syrup (about 10% sugar) NCHFP uses 6 1/2 cups of water and 3/4 cup of sugar for a load of 9 pint jars, or 10 1/2 cups of water and 1 1/4 cups of sugar for 7 quart jars. [T:ACI-192]

## Packing

Hot pack: put the drained fruit in syrup, water or juice in a large saucepan and bring to a boil. [T:ACI-220]

Fill the jars with hot fruit and cooking liquid, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace, and place the halves in layers, cut side down. [T:ACI-220]

Raw pack: fill the jars with raw fruit, cut side down, and add hot water, juice or syrup, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:ACI-221]

NCHFP notes that raw packs make poor quality apricots. [T:ACI-219]

For both packs, put on the lids and process. [T:ACI-221]

## Processing

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process hot-packed pints for 20 minutes and quarts for 25, and raw-packed pints for 25 minutes and quarts for 30. [T:ACI-072,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–3000 ft (above 300 m up to 900 m): hot pack, pints 25 minutes and quarts 30; raw pack, pints 30 minutes and quarts 35. [T:ACI-222,ACI-176]

At 3001–6000 ft (above 900 m up to 1800 m): hot pack, pints 30 minutes and quarts 35; raw pack, pints 35 minutes and quarts 40. [T:ACI-223,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m): hot pack, pints 35 minutes and quarts 40; raw pack, pints 40 minutes and quarts 45. [T:ACI-224,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 3000 ft becomes 900 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

NCHFP times are for US jar sizes: a pint holds about 473 ml and a quart about 946 ml. [T:ACI-174]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): for a jar of up to 473 ml use the pint time, and for a larger jar of up to 946 ml, for example 0.5 l or 0.9 l, use the quart time. [T:ACI-175]

This NCHFP recipe gives no time for jars larger than a quart, such as 1 l, so JarAtlas recommends smaller jars. [T:ACI-175]
