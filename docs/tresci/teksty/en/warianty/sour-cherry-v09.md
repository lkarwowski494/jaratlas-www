# Whole sour cherries in water, no sugar (NCHFP)

This card describes the NCHFP method for canning whole sour cherries in water in a boiling-water bath. [T:ACI-243,ACI-237]

## Preparing

NCHFP needs on average 17.5 pounds (about 7.9 kg) of cherries for a canner load of 7 quart jars and 11 pounds (about 5 kg) for 9 pint jars. [T:ACI-324]

Choose bright, evenly colored, mature cherries of a quality fit for eating fresh or cooking. [T:ACI-235]

Remove the stems and wash the cherries; removing the pits is optional. [T:ACI-236]

Keep pitted cherries in water with ascorbic acid so they do not darken at the stem end. [T:ACI-236]

If you leave the pits in, prick the skin of each cherry on opposite sides with a clean needle so it does not split. [T:ACI-236]

NCHFP allows water, apple juice, white grape juice or syrup as the liquid; this card uses water, without sugar. [T:ACI-237]

Syrup helps keep the flavor, color and shape of the fruit, but it does not prevent spoilage; fruit can also be packed in juice or water. [T:ACI-067,ACI-237]

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

## Packing

Hot pack: put the drained cherries in a large saucepan, add 1/2 cup of water for each quart (about 0.95 l) of fruit and bring to a boil. [T:ACI-238]

Fill the jars with the cherries and cooking liquid, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:ACI-238]

Raw pack: pour 1/2 cup of hot water into each jar, then fill the jar with drained cherries, shaking it gently as you fill. [T:ACI-239]

Add more hot water, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:ACI-239]

For both packs, put on the lids and process. [T:ACI-239]

## Processing

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process hot-packed pints for 15 minutes and quarts for 20, and raw-packed pints or quarts for 25 minutes. [T:ACI-070,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–3000 ft (above 300 m up to 900 m): hot pack, pints 20 minutes and quarts 25; raw pack, pints or quarts 30 minutes. [T:ACI-240,ACI-176]

At 3001–6000 ft (above 900 m up to 1800 m): hot pack, pints 20 minutes and quarts 30; raw pack, pints or quarts 35 minutes. [T:ACI-241,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m): hot pack, pints 25 minutes and quarts 35; raw pack, pints or quarts 40 minutes. [T:ACI-242,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 3000 ft becomes 900 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

NCHFP times are for US jar sizes: a pint holds about 473 ml and a quart about 946 ml. [T:ACI-174]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): for a jar of up to 473 ml use the pint time, and for a larger jar of up to 946 ml, for example 0.5 l or 0.9 l, use the quart time. [T:ACI-175]

This NCHFP recipe gives no time for jars larger than a quart, such as 1 l, so JarAtlas recommends smaller jars. [T:ACI-175]

## Cooling and storing

When you take the hot jars out of the canner, do not retighten the lids or bands; this can cut through the gasket and cause seal failures. [T:SLO-210]

In raw-packed jars the food and liquid level will be noticeably lower after cooling. [T:SLO-210]

If any jar loses a lot of liquid during processing, do not open it to add more. [T:SLO-210]

Cool the jars at room temperature for 12 to 24 hours, then remove only the screw bands (the metal rings of two-piece lids), leaving the flat lids on the jars, and test the seal: press the middle of the lid, and if it springs up when you release it, the jar is not sealed. [T:SLO-196,SLO-212]

This seal test and the whole method assume Mason-type canning jars with two-piece lids; NCHFP does not recommend jars whose mouths cannot be sealed with two-piece canning lids for canning any food at home. [T:SLO-223]

A sealed lid is concave, curved down slightly in the centre; if the centre is flat or bulging, the jar may not be sealed. [T:SLO-196]

If a jar has not sealed at this check, keep it in the refrigerator and eat it within several days. [T:SLO-197]

Store sealed jars in a clean, cool, dark, dry place, not above 95 °F (35 °C) and not near hot pipes, a stove or a furnace, in an uninsulated attic or in direct sunlight; in such conditions the food may spoil. [T:SLO-198]

## When to throw it out

Before using a stored jar, check that the lid is tight and holds a vacuum: a good seal has a concave centre. [T:SLO-191]

Look for streaks of dried food running from the top of the jar, rising air bubbles and unnatural colour. [T:SLO-191]

When opening the jar, check for an unnatural smell, spurting liquid and cotton-like mould on the food or under the lid. [T:SLO-191]

Do not taste food from a jar with an unsealed lid or with signs of spoilage. [T:SLO-190]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): throw out such a jar. [T:SLO-190]
