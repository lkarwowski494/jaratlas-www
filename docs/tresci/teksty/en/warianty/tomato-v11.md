# Tomato paste (NCHFP)

This card describes the NCHFP recipe for tomato paste: paste-type tomatoes cooked down, sieved, acidified with citric acid and cooked down again, hot packed in half-pint jars and processed in a boiling-water bath. [T:ACI-300,ACI-302,ACI-304]

## Why you must not change the recipe

The FDA list gives tomatoes a pH of about 4.30–4.90, so some tomatoes have a pH slightly above 4.6. [T:ACI-093,ACI-008]

Tomatoes canned as an acid food must be acidified to pH 4.6 or lower. [T:ACI-008]

Do not use tomatoes from dead or frost-killed vines. [T:ACI-049]

In this recipe the citric acid must be stirred into the sieved tomatoes before they are cooked down into paste; adding it to the jars, as with other canned tomato products, does not spread the acid evenly through the paste. [T:ACI-302]

Keep the ratio of citric acid to pounds of tomatoes the same. [T:ACI-302]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): do not add other vegetables and do not change the amounts of tomatoes or citric acid; mixtures of low-acid and acid foods have a pH above 4.6 unless the recipe includes enough acid, and this recipe is tested only in the proportions given. [T:ACI-265,ACI-006]

## Ingredients

For about 8 or 9 half-pint jars NCHFP lists 14 pounds (about 6.35 kg) of Roma or paste-type tomatoes, 1 teaspoon of citric acid, 2 bay leaves, 1 teaspoon of canning or pickling salt and, if you like, 1 clove of garlic. [T:ACI-300]

## Cooking

Wash and rinse the half-pint jars and keep them hot until filling; prepare the lids according to the manufacturer. [T:ACI-301]

Rinse the tomatoes thoroughly under running water and remove the cores; do not peel them. [T:ACI-301]

Chop the tomatoes into 1/2- to 3/4-inch (about 1.3–1.9 cm) pieces, put them in a stockpot, cover and bring to a boil, stirring as needed so they do not burn. [T:ACI-301]

Reduce the heat, remove the lid and simmer for about 1 hour, stirring often, until the volume is reduced by half. [T:ACI-301]

Press the cooked tomatoes through a fine sieve or a food mill with a fine blade; do not use a blender or food processor, because they work unwanted air into the tomatoes. [T:ACI-301]

Return the sieved tomatoes to the pot and stir in the citric acid thoroughly. [T:ACI-302]

Stir in any or all of the salt, bay leaves and garlic, if you use them. [T:ACI-302]

Keep cooking slowly over medium heat, uncovered, for about 2 1/2 hours, stirring often, until the paste is thick enough to round up on a spoon and the volume is reduced by half again. [T:ACI-302]

Take care: the paste can spatter and burn your skin while you stir. [T:ACI-302]

## Filling and processing

Remove the bay leaves and garlic, if used. [T:ACI-303]

Fill the hot paste into clean, hot half-pint jars, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:ACI-303]

Remove air bubbles, adjust the headspace if needed, wipe the jar rims with a damp clean paper towel and put on the lids. [T:ACI-303]

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process half-pint jars in a boiling-water bath for 45 minutes. [T:ACI-304,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–3000 ft (above 300 m up to 900 m) process them for 50 minutes. [T:ACI-317,ACI-176]

At 3001–6000 ft (above 900 m up to 1800 m) process them for 55 minutes. [T:ACI-318,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 60 minutes. [T:ACI-319,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 3000 ft becomes 900 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

The NCHFP table gives times only for half-pint jars. [T:ACI-304]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): half a pint is about 236.6 ml, so a 0.25 l or 0.5 l jar has no tested time in this recipe; use jars of up to 236 ml, for example 0.2 l. [T:ACI-294,ACI-174]

Let the jars cool undisturbed for 12 to 24 hours, then check that the lids have sealed. [T:ACI-303]
