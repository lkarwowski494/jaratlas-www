# Sauerkraut, and why there is no caraway amount here (NCHFP recipe)

This card describes the NCHFP recipe for sauerkraut, which lists only cabbage and canning or pickling salt. [T:FER-107]

## Safety first

Do not make sauerkraut with a reduced amount of salt. [T:FER-011]

According to Oregon State University, slimy sauerkraut is spoiled and must be discarded, and if there is a lot of mold the whole batch must be discarded. [T:FER-055]

## What this card covers

The NCHFP recipe does not list caraway or any other spice. [T:FER-107]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): JarAtlas has no tested source for fermenting cabbage with caraway, so this card gives no amount of caraway and does not recommend additions outside the recipe. [T:FER-108]

Do not change the amounts of cabbage or salt. [T:FER-108,FER-011]

## Ingredients

NCHFP uses 3/4 cup of canning salt for 25 pounds of cabbage, working in batches of 5 pounds of cabbage with 3 tablespoons of salt. [T:FER-029]

According to the University of Wisconsin this is 2.25–2.5% salt by weight, and the salt should be non-iodized canning salt. [T:FER-031]

## Fermenting

Throughout fermentation the cabbage must stay 1–2 inches (about 2.5–5 cm) under the brine. [T:FER-021]

If the juice does not cover the cabbage, add boiled and cooled brine made with 1.5 tablespoons of salt per quart of water. [T:FER-030]

At 21–24 °C full fermentation takes about 3–4 weeks, and at 16–18 °C it may take 5–6 weeks. [T:FER-034]

Below 60 °F (16 °C) the cabbage may not ferment, and above 75 °F (24 °C) it may become soft. [T:FER-034]

If you weigh the cabbage down with a brine-filled bag, do not disturb the container until fermentation is complete, when bubbling stops. [T:FER-035]

If you weigh it down with jars, check the sauerkraut 2–3 times a week and remove scum if it forms. [T:FER-035]

## Storing

Fully fermented sauerkraut may be kept tightly covered in the refrigerator for several months, or it may be canned. [T:FER-036]

For hot pack, slowly bring the sauerkraut and its juice to a boil, stirring often, take it off the heat and pack the jars fairly firmly with sauerkraut and juice. [T:FER-097]

For raw pack, pack the jars firmly with sauerkraut and cover it with juice. [T:FER-097]

Either way, leave 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:FER-097]

Water-bath canning at 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) above sea level takes 10 minutes for pint jars and 15 minutes for quart jars when hot packed, and 20 minutes for pints and 25 minutes for quarts when raw packed. [T:FER-037,ACI-176]

At 1001–3000 ft (above 300 m up to 900 m): hot pack 15 minutes for pints and 20 for quarts, raw pack 25 and 30 minutes. [T:FER-098,ACI-176]

At 3001–6000 ft (above 900 m up to 1800 m): hot pack 15 minutes for pints and 20 for quarts, raw pack 30 and 35 minutes. [T:FER-100,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m): hot pack 20 minutes for pints and 25 for quarts, raw pack 35 and 40 minutes. [T:FER-101,ACI-176]

Keep the water at least 1 inch (about 2.5 cm) above the jar tops during processing, and 2 inches (about 5 cm) above the jars if the processing time is over 30 minutes; if needed, add boiling water around the jars, not onto them. [T:ACI-953]

Start timing only once the water is boiling. [T:ACI-954]

If the water stops boiling at any time, turn the heat to its highest setting, bring the water back to a vigorous boil and time the whole process again from the beginning. [T:ACI-947]

When the time is up, turn off the heat, remove the canner lid and wait 5 minutes before taking the jars out; NCHFP says this wait is not needed for food safety when USDA or University of Georgia times are used. [T:ACI-955]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m, 3000 ft becomes 900 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

NCHFP times are for US jar sizes: a pint holds about 473 ml and a quart about 946 ml. [T:ACI-174]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): for a jar of up to 473 ml use the pint time, and for a larger jar of up to 946 ml, for example 0.5 l or 0.9 l, use the quart time. [T:ACI-175]

This NCHFP recipe gives no time for jars larger than a quart, such as 1 l, so JarAtlas recommends smaller jars. [T:ACI-175]

## When to throw it out

According to Oregon State University, small spots of mold can be removed, but if there is a lot of mold the whole batch must be discarded. [T:FER-055]

Slimy sauerkraut is spoiled, so discard it. [T:FER-055]

Discard any rotting parts. [T:FER-055]
