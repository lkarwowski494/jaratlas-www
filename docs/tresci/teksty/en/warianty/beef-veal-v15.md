# Frozen beef and veal (NCHFP)

This card describes freezing fresh meat (beef, veal) according to the NCHFP, at 0 °F (−18 °C) or lower. [T:SLO-412,SUS-050]

## Choosing and preparing

Freeze only high-quality fresh meat. [T:SLO-412]

Meat from the store can be frozen as is, or cut. [T:SLO-411]

Cool home-slaughtered meat below 40 °F (about 4 °C) within 24 hours, best to 32–36 °F (about 0–2 °C), so it does not sour or spoil. [T:SLO-411]

Variety meats such as liver, heart and sweetbreads are ready to wrap and freeze once cold. [T:SLO-411]

Veal is ready to cut and freeze after 24 hours of chilling. [T:SLO-411]

## Packaging

Package meat in meal-size portions and remove as many bones as possible. [T:SLO-414]

Place two layers of freezer paper or wrap between slices or patties so they separate easily when frozen; this also speeds thawing. [T:SLO-414]

Wrap meat in freezer paper or wrap using the drugstore or butcher wrap; ground meat, stew meat and small portions can be frozen in freezer bags or containers. [T:SLO-413]

Over-wrap store-bought meat, because its clear packaging does not protect against moisture loss. [T:SLO-413]

If it will stay in its original package longer than 2 months, overwrap it airtight with heavy-duty foil, plastic wrap or freezer paper, or place it in a bag. [T:SLO-413]

Use packaging that is moisture- and vapor-resistant, durable and leakproof, and does not become brittle or crack in the cold. [T:SLO-251]

Pack tightly, leaving as little air as possible, and press as much air as possible out of freezer bags before sealing. [T:SLO-250,SLO-251]

Label each package with the name of the product, any added ingredients, the packaging date, the number of servings or amount, and the form, such as steaks or ground meat. [T:SLO-250]

## Freezing and storage

Freeze at 0 °F (−18 °C) or lower. [T:SUS-050]

To speed up freezing, set the freezer to −10 °F (about −23 °C) or lower about 24 hours in advance. [T:SUS-051]

Add to the freezer at one time only as much food as will freeze within 24 hours, because overloading slows freezing and lowers quality. [T:SLO-402]

Spread new, warm packages with space between them, and stack them close together only once they are frozen. [T:SLO-402]

According to Clemson University (HGIC), freeze as quickly as possible, because slow freezing forms large ice crystals that damage cells, and the meat loses juiciness when thawed. [T:SLO-403]

According to the same source, a refrigerator freezing compartment that cannot hold 0 °F (−18 °C) is for short-term storage only. [T:SLO-403]

Keep the freezer at 0 °F (−18 °C) or lower, because at higher temperatures food loses quality faster; keep a freezer thermometer in it and check it often. [T:SLO-255]

Freezing does not kill all bacteria, yeasts and molds; it only stops them multiplying while the food is held at 0 °F (−18 °C) or lower, and after thawing they can multiply again. [T:SLO-401]

Food kept constantly at 0 °F (−18 °C) stays safe indefinitely, and the recommended storage times are for quality only: after them the food is still safe but lower in flavor, color and texture. [T:SLO-400]

Freezer burn, grayish-brown leathery spots, is surface drying of poorly wrapped food caused by air; it does not make food unsafe, it only lowers quality, and the burned spots can be cut away. [T:SLO-404]

For best quality, keep to the shorter time and use the oldest packages first. [T:SLO-261]

According to the FDA, fresh beef, veal, lamb and pork keep their quality in the freezer for: steaks 6–12 months, chops 4–6 months, roasts 4–12 months, variety meats 3–4 months, ground and stew meat 3–4 months. [T:SLO-417]

## Thawing and refreezing

Thaw best in the refrigerator, in the original wrapping. [T:SLO-405]

Faster: in waterproof wrapping in cold water that you change at least every 30 minutes so it stays cold. [T:SLO-405]

Thaw in the microwave only if you will cook it immediately afterwards. [T:SLO-405]

Never thaw on the kitchen counter, in a garage, basement, car or outdoors. [T:SLO-405]

Thawing in the refrigerator needs planning: even a small amount of ground meat or boneless chicken breasts needs a full day, and a large item such as a turkey needs at least 1 day (24 hours) for every 5 pounds (about 2.3 kg). [T:SLO-406]

Meat, fish and poultry can be cooked straight from the freezer, but extra time is needed, for large roasts up to 1 1/2 times as long. [T:SLO-407]

For best quality, cook thawed meat or fish right away. [T:SLO-407]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): refreeze without cooking only what thawed in the refrigerator, and put it back in the freezer within one to two days. [T:SLO-409]

Cook anything you thawed in cold water or in the microwave first; only then may you freeze it. [T:SLO-409]

Do not refreeze by any method anything that was in the danger zone for more than 2 hours (1 hour when the temperature is above 90 °F, about 32 °C); throw it out. [T:SLO-409]

## When to throw it away

Do not keep perishable food out of the refrigerator for more than 2 hours, or for more than 1 hour when the temperature is above 90 °F (about 32 °C). [T:SLO-408]

Food is safe while it stays frozen, but once it thaws and warms above 40 °F (about 4 °C), bacteria present before freezing can start to multiply. [T:SUS-059]

During a power outage keep the freezer closed: according to the FDA, a full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours, a half-full one for 24 hours. [T:SLO-259]

According to the NCHFP, food stays frozen longer if the freezer is full, unopened, in a cool place and well insulated; it can be covered with blankets, away from the compressor. [T:SLO-410]

JarAtlas rule (cautious) after a freezer failure: throw away any package that has no ice crystals and is above 40 °F (about 4 °C), or that you cannot check. [T:SLO-260]

The FDA also says to throw away perishable food that has been at room temperature for 2 hours or more, or 1 hour when it is above 90 °F (about 32 °C) outside. [T:SLO-260]
