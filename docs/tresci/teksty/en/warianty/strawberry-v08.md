# Strawberry preserves (NCHFP)

This card describes the NCHFP recipe for strawberry preserves: strawberries cooked with sugar, without added pectin. [T:SLO-164,SLO-166,SLO-167]

## Ingredients

NCHFP describes preserves as small, whole fruit or uniform-size pieces in a clear, slightly gelled syrup. [T:SLO-180]

NCHFP uses 6 cups of prepared strawberries (about 2 quart boxes) and 4.5 cups of sugar, which makes about 4 half-pint jars. [T:SLO-164]

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

NCHFP warns not to reduce the sugar in traditional recipes: too little sugar prevents gelling and may allow yeasts and molds to grow. [T:SLO-001]

## Preparing

Sterilize the jars and prepare two-piece lids according to the manufacturer's directions. [T:SLO-165]

NCHFP puts the empty jars right side up on the rack in a boiling-water canner and fills the canner and jars with hot (not boiling) water to 1 inch (about 2.5 cm) above the tops of the jars. [T:SLO-120]

To sterilize empty jars, NCHFP boils them for 10 minutes below 1000 ft, and above that 1 minute longer for each additional thousand feet. [T:SLO-118]

JarAtlas rule for metres: boil the jars for 10 minutes up to 300 m, and add 1 minute for every further 300 m or part of it. [T:SLO-119]

Choose large, firm, tart strawberries. [T:SLO-165]

Wash and drain the berries and remove the caps. [T:SLO-165]

## Making the preserves

Layer the fruit and sugar alternately and let them stand for 8 to 10 hours or overnight in the refrigerator or another cool place. [T:SLO-166]

Heat the fruit mixture to a boil, stirring gently. [T:SLO-167]

Boil rapidly, stirring as needed so it does not stick. [T:SLO-167]

Cook to 9 °F (5 °C) above the boiling point of water, or until the syrup is somewhat thick, about 15 to 20 minutes. [T:SLO-167]

Remove from the heat and skim. [T:SLO-167]

## Canning

Just before filling, remove and drain the hot sterilized jars one at a time. [T:SLO-120]

Fill the hot preserves immediately into hot, sterile jars, leaving 1/4 inch (about 0.6 cm) of headspace. [T:SLO-168]

Wipe the jar rims with a damp clean paper towel, put on two-piece metal lids and process in a boiling-water canner. [T:SLO-168]

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process half-pint and pint jars for 5 minutes. [T:SLO-169,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–6000 ft (above 300 m up to 1800 m) process them for 10 minutes. [T:SLO-170,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 15 minutes. [T:SLO-171,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

The NCHFP table gives times only for half-pint and pint jars. [T:SLO-169]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): a pint holds about 473 ml, so a 0.5 l jar has no tested time in this recipe; use jars of up to 473 ml. [T:ACI-179,ACI-174]

## Storing

NCHFP says opened home-canned jams and jellies should be kept in the refrigerator at 40 °F (about 4 °C) or lower; JarAtlas applies this to preserves too. [T:SLO-018]

## When to throw it out

NCHFP says to discard jams and jellies with mold on them, and USDA and microbiologists advise against scooping out the mold and eating the rest; JarAtlas applies this to preserves too. [T:SLO-012]

Check an opened jar regularly for signs of spoilage such as mold, yeast or an off odor, including a fermented, yeasty or alcohol smell; if you find any, throw out the whole jar; NCHFP gives this for jams and jellies, and JarAtlas applies it to preserves too. [T:SLO-181,SLO-013]
