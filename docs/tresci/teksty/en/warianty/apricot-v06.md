# Apricot jam without added pectin (NCHFP)

This card describes the NCHFP recipe for apricot jam without added pectin: crushed, peeled apricots cooked with lemon juice and sugar until thick and processed in a boiling-water bath. [T:ACI-305,ACI-306,ACI-307]

## !

- Apricot pH varies: the US FDA list gives 3.30–4.80, so the upper end is above 4.6, the limit for acid foods. [T:ACI-135,ACI-308,ACI-001] This recipe is tested with lemon juice: do not leave it out or reduce it, and do not reduce the sugar. [T:ACI-308,SLO-001]

## Ingredients

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

For about 10 half-pint jars NCHFP uses 2 quarts (about 1.9 l) of crushed, peeled apricots, 1/4 cup of lemon juice and 6 cups of sugar. [T:ACI-305]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): use bottled lemon juice in the amount given; NCHFP notes, in its lemon curd recipe, that fresh lemon juice can vary in acidity. [T:ACI-314,SLO-039]

NCHFP warns not to reduce the sugar in traditional recipes: too little sugar prevents gelling and may allow yeasts and molds to grow. [T:SLO-001]

## Preparing

Sterilize the jars. [T:ACI-306]

NCHFP puts the empty jars right side up on the rack in a boiling-water canner and fills the canner and jars with hot (not boiling) water to 1 inch (about 2.5 cm) above the tops of the jars. [T:SLO-120]

To sterilize empty jars, NCHFP boils them for 10 minutes below 1000 ft, and above that 1 minute longer for each additional thousand feet. [T:SLO-118]

JarAtlas rule for metres: boil the jars for 10 minutes up to 300 m, and add 1 minute for every further 300 m or part of it. [T:SLO-119]

To peel apricots, NCHFP dips the washed fruit in boiling water for 30–60 seconds until the skins loosen, dips it quickly in cold water and slips off the skins. [T:ACI-218]

## Making the jam

Combine all the ingredients and bring slowly to a boil, stirring occasionally until the sugar dissolves. [T:ACI-306]

Cook rapidly until thick, about 25 minutes; as the mixture thickens, stir often so it does not stick. [T:ACI-306]

Remove from the heat. [T:ACI-306]

## Canning

Just before filling, remove and drain the hot sterilized jars one at a time. [T:SLO-120]

Fill the hot jam into hot, sterile jars, leaving 1/4 inch (about 0.6 cm) of headspace. [T:ACI-306]

Wipe the jar rims with a damp clean paper towel, put on two-piece metal lids and process in a boiling-water canner. [T:ACI-306]

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process half-pint and pint jars for 5 minutes. [T:ACI-307,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–6000 ft (above 300 m up to 1800 m) process them for 10 minutes. [T:ACI-320,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 15 minutes. [T:ACI-321,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

The NCHFP table gives times only for half-pint and pint jars. [T:ACI-307]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): a pint holds about 473 ml, so a 0.5 l jar has no tested time in this recipe; use jars of up to 473 ml. [T:ACI-179,ACI-174]

## Storing

Keep opened jam in the refrigerator at 40 °F (about 4 °C) or lower. [T:SLO-018]

Regular full-sugar cooked jams like this one are best kept in the refrigerator for 1 month after opening. [T:SLO-018]

Discard jam with mold on it; USDA and microbiologists advise against scooping out the mold and eating the rest. [T:SLO-012]

Check an opened jar regularly for signs of spoilage such as mold, yeast or an off odor, including a fermented, yeasty or alcohol smell; if you find any, throw out the whole jar. [T:SLO-181,SLO-013]
