# Corncob jelly with pectin (NCHFP)

This card describes the NCHFP recipe for corncob jelly: a jelly made from the liquid of boiled red field corn cobs with powdered pectin and sugar, processed in a boiling-water bath. [T:ACI-275,ACI-278,ACI-279]

## Why you must follow the recipe exactly

Sweet corn has a pH of 7.3 according to an FDA table of approximate values. [T:SLO-035]

NCHFP tested this recipe with SURE-JELL Premium Fruit Pectin and states that the recipe, as tested, produces an acidic jelly. [T:ACI-282]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): JarAtlas has no data for sweet corn cobs or for other substitutions, so follow the recipe exactly: red field corn cobs, the powdered pectin used in the test (SURE-JELL Premium Fruit Pectin, 1 3/4 ounces, about 50 g) and unchanged amounts. [T:ACI-283]

NCHFP warns not to reduce the sugar in traditional recipes: too little sugar prevents gelling and may allow yeasts and molds to grow. [T:SLO-001]

## Ingredients

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

For the corncob liquid NCHFP uses 12 (a dozen) medium-sized fresh red corncobs from field corn, cobs only, and 2 quarts (about 1.9 l) of water. [T:ACI-275]

For the jelly: 3 cups of corncob liquid, 1 package (1 3/4 ounces) of powdered pectin and 3 cups of sugar; this makes about 4 half-pint jars. [T:ACI-275]

## Making the corncob liquid

Wash the corncobs and cut them into 4-inch (about 10 cm) lengths. [T:ACI-276]

Put them in a large stockpot, add 2 quarts of water or enough to cover them, and bring to a boil. [T:ACI-276]

Reduce the heat and boil slowly for 35 to 40 minutes. [T:ACI-276]

Strain the liquid through a double layer of cheesecloth or a jelly bag and let it drip through; do not press or squeeze the bag or cloth. [T:ACI-276]

## Making the jelly

Measure 3 cups of corncob liquid into a large saucepot; if needed, add water to make 3 cups. [T:ACI-277]

Stir in the pectin and bring to a boil. [T:ACI-277]

Add all the sugar at once and, stirring, bring the mixture back to a full rolling boil. [T:ACI-277]

Boil for 5 minutes, remove from the heat and skim off the foam quickly. [T:ACI-277]

## Filling and processing

JarAtlas uses the NCHFP option without pre-sterilizing the jars: wash and rinse the jars in hot water and keep them hot until filling; the processing time is then longer. [T:ACI-279]

Pour the hot jelly immediately into the hot jars, leaving 1/4 inch (about 0.6 cm) of headspace. [T:ACI-278]

Wipe the jar rims with a damp clean paper towel, put on two-piece metal lids and process in a boiling-water canner. [T:ACI-278]

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process half-pint and pint jars in a boiling-water bath for 10 minutes. [T:ACI-279,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–6000 ft (above 300 m up to 1800 m) process them for 15 minutes. [T:ACI-280,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 20 minutes. [T:ACI-281,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

The NCHFP table gives times only for half-pint and pint jars. [T:ACI-279]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): a pint holds about 473 ml, so a 0.5 l jar has no tested time in this recipe; use jars of up to 473 ml. [T:ACI-179,ACI-174]

## When to throw it out

Discard jelly with mold on it; USDA and microbiologists advise against scooping out the mold and eating the rest. [T:SLO-012]

Keep opened jelly in the refrigerator at 40 °F (about 4 °C) or lower. [T:SLO-018]

Regular full-sugar cooked jellies made with added pectin, like this one, are best kept in the refrigerator for 1 month after opening. [T:SLO-018]

Opening the jar often or leaving it out at room temperature for long periods shortens this time; you can spoon the amount you need into a bowl and put the jar back in the refrigerator quickly. [T:SLO-213]

Keep the opened jar in the refrigerator until it is used up and check it often for signs of spoilage, such as mould, yeast growth or a fermented, alcoholic or yeasty smell; if you see any of them, throw the jelly out at once. [T:SLO-213]

Check an opened jar regularly for signs of spoilage such as mold, yeast or an off odor, including a fermented, yeasty or alcohol smell; if you find any, throw out the whole jar. [T:SLO-181,SLO-013]
