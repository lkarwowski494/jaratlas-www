# Figs in balsamic vinegar: why there is no recipe here

There is no recipe here for figs in balsamic vinegar: JarAtlas has no recipe for them from a checked source. [T:-]

## !

- A boiling-water bath is suitable only for acid foods, such as fruits, pickles, jams and fruit butters, and only with the research-based process times of the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning. [T:SZY-054]
- Do not change the proportions of vinegar, food and water and do not use vinegar of unknown acidity; use only recipes with tested proportions. [T:ACI-016]
- For quick pickles, University of Wisconsin Extension advises: use only vinegar labelled 5% acidity; white and cider vinegar can be used interchangeably; do not use wine vinegars, homemade vinegar or lemon juice. [T:ACI-509]

## Why

The FDA list gives Calamyrna figs a pH of about 5.05–5.98. [T:ACI-154]

Figs have pH values slightly above 4.6, so figs canned as an acid food must first be acidified to a pH of 4.6 or lower. [T:ACI-008]

White distilled vinegar and cider vinegar of 5% acidity are recommended for pickles. [T:ACI-018]

Using a boiling-water bath for low-acid foods poses a real risk of botulism poisoning. [T:SZY-054]

Without a tested recipe, there is no checked process time or vinegar proportion for figs in balsamic vinegar. [T:-]
