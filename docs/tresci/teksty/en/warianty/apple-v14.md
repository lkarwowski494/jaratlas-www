# Apple chutney with golden raisins (NCHFP)

This card describes the NCHFP recipe for apple chutney: chopped tart apples with onion, sweet and hot peppers, golden raisins, brown sugar, spices and cider vinegar, simmered until thick, hot packed and processed in a boiling-water bath. [T:SLO-627,SLO-629,SLO-630]

## Why you must not change the recipe

The FDA list gives eating apples a pH of about 3.30–4.00. [T:ACI-126]

NCHFP keeps its apple recipes but advises using fresh, sound apples, and if you are unsure of their pH, refrigerate or freeze apple products instead of storing them at room temperature. [T:SLO-048]

Most mixtures of low-acid and acid foods have a pH above 4.6 unless the recipe includes enough lemon juice, citric acid or vinegar, so do not alter the vinegar, food or water proportions and do not use vinegar of unknown acidity. [T:ACI-006,ACI-016]

The whole product must contain a minimum, uniform level of acid so that botulism bacteria cannot grow. [T:ACI-017]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): do not change the amounts of vinegar, apples, onions or sweet peppers and do not add other vegetables; the only change NCHFP allows is the hot pepper, described below; the recipe is tested only in the proportions given. [T:ACI-265,ACI-646]

NCHFP allows another type of hot pepper as long as you do not exceed the listed measurement, or you may leave the hot pepper out. [T:ACI-646]

White distilled vinegar and cider vinegar of 5% acidity are recommended for pickles. [T:ACI-018]

Penn State Extension advises vinegar of 5–7% acidity for home food preservation and advises against diluting vinegar with water unless a research-tested recipe says so. [T:ACI-209,ACI-210]

JarAtlas rule (cautious, for readers outside the USA): use the cider vinegar the recipe lists, labelled 5% or 6% acidity; do not use 10% vinegar and do not dilute it with water to make 5% vinegar; add only the water the recipe lists. [T:ACI-211,ACI-018]

## Ingredients

NCHFP uses US measures: a cup holds about 237 ml. [T:ACI-195]

For about 6 pint jars NCHFP lists 16 cups (4 quarts) of pared, cored and chopped tart apples (about 10 medium), 1 cup of chopped onions, 1 cup of chopped sweet red bell peppers (about 1 medium) and 2 teaspoons of seeded, finely chopped red Serrano pepper (about 2 to 4 peppers). [T:SLO-627]

It also lists 12 ounces (about 340 g) of seedless golden raisins, 4 cups of light brown sugar, 3 tablespoons of mustard seed, 2 tablespoons of ground ginger, 2 teaspoons of ground allspice, 2 teaspoons of canning salt, 1 crushed clove of garlic and 4 cups of cider vinegar (5%). [T:SLO-627]

Wear plastic or rubber gloves and do not touch your face while handling or cutting hot peppers. [T:SLO-628]

If you do not wear gloves, wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water before touching your face or eyes. [T:SLO-628]

## Cooking and filling

Wash and rinse pint or half-pint canning jars and keep them hot until you fill them; prepare the lids and ring bands according to the manufacturer's directions. [T:SLO-629]

Combine all the prepared ingredients in a large stockpot and bring to a boil. [T:SLO-629]

Reduce the heat and simmer until thickened, about 45 minutes; as the mixture thickens, stir often so it does not stick. [T:SLO-629]

Fill the hot chutney into hot jars, leaving 1/2 inch (about 1.3 cm) of headspace. [T:SLO-629]

Remove air bubbles and adjust the headspace if needed. [T:SLO-629]

Wipe the jar rims with a damp clean paper towel, put on the lids and process. [T:SLO-629]

## Processing

At 0–1000 ft (up to 300 m) process pint jars in a boiling-water bath for 10 minutes. [T:SLO-630,ACI-176]

Above 1000 ft the food may spoil if the time is not increased. [T:ACI-015]

At 1001–6000 ft (above 300 m up to 1800 m) process them for 15 minutes. [T:SLO-631,ACI-176]

Above 6000 ft (above 1800 m) process them for 20 minutes. [T:SLO-632,ACI-176]

The card converts feet to metres and rounds the limits down, towards the longer time: 1000 ft becomes 300 m and 6000 ft becomes 1800 m. [T:ACI-176]

Shortening the time or cooling jars in cold water can also lead to spoilage. [T:ACI-015]

The NCHFP table gives times only for pint jars; the procedure also mentions half-pint jars. [T:SLO-630,SLO-629]

JarAtlas rule (NCHFP does not test metric jars): a pint holds about 473 ml, so a 0.5 l jar has no tested time in this recipe; use jars of up to 473 ml. [T:ACI-179,ACI-174]

## Cooling and storing

When you take the hot jars out of the canner, do not retighten the lids or bands; this can cut through the gasket and cause seal failures. [T:SLO-210]

Cool the jars at room temperature for 12 to 24 hours, then remove only the screw bands (the metal rings of two-piece lids), leaving the flat lids on the jars, and test the seal: press the middle of the lid, and if it springs up when you release it, the jar is not sealed. [T:SLO-196,SLO-212]

NCHFP calls Mason-type canning jars with self-sealing two-piece lids (a flat lid and a screw band) the best choice, and this seal test and the method on this card assume them; NCHFP does not recommend other commercial jars whose mouths cannot be sealed with two-piece canning lids for canning any food at home. [T:SLO-223,SLO-212]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): use this card only with jars that take two-piece canning lids; it gives no method for twist-off jars or jars with glass lids, so do not use them for this recipe. [T:SLO-223]

A sealed lid is concave, curved down slightly in the centre; if the centre is flat or bulging, the jar may not be sealed. [T:SLO-196]

If a jar has not sealed at this check, keep it in the refrigerator and eat it within several days. [T:SLO-197]

Store sealed jars in a clean, cool, dark, dry place, not above 95 °F (35 °C) and not near hot pipes, a stove or a furnace, in an uninsulated attic or in direct sunlight; in such conditions the food may spoil. [T:SLO-198]

Refrigerate any chutney left over from filling the jars and eat it fresh. [T:SLO-633]

Once a jar is opened, keep it in the refrigerator. [T:SLO-633]

## When to throw it out

Before using a stored jar, check that the lid is tight and holds a vacuum: a good seal has a concave centre. [T:SLO-191]

Look for streaks of dried food running from the top of the jar, rising air bubbles and unnatural colour. [T:SLO-191]

When opening the jar, check for an unnatural smell, spurting liquid and cotton-like mould on the food or under the lid. [T:SLO-191]

Do not taste food from a jar with an unsealed lid or with signs of spoilage. [T:SLO-190]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): throw out such a jar. [T:SLO-190]
