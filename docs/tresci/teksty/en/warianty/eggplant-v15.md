# Dried eggplant slices (CSU)

This card describes how to dry eggplant slices in a dehydrator or oven at 140 °F (60 °C), after blanching. [T:SUS-001,SUS-116]

## Choosing and preparing

Wash and trim the eggplant, then cut it into 1/4-inch (about 6 mm) slices. [T:SUS-116]

Blanch the slices for 4 minutes. [T:SUS-116]

MSU rates eggplant as “poor to fair” for drying; MSU gives no reason for this rating. [T:SUS-121]

## Blanching

Vegetables are blanched before drying to stop enzymes and increase the destruction of microorganisms. [T:SUS-014]

Put at most 1 quart (about 0.95 l) of vegetables in a cheesecloth bag into boiling water; start timing when they go in and keep the water boiling the whole time. [T:SUS-106]

Then cool the vegetables in very cold water for the same time as they were blanched, and drain them. [T:SUS-106]

The blanching times on this card are for 3000–5000 feet (about 900–1500 m); they are slightly shorter at lower altitudes and slightly longer at higher altitudes or for large amounts of vegetables. [T:SUS-105]

## Drying

Arrange the pretreated vegetables on trays in a single or thin layer, no more than 1/2 inch (about 13 mm) deep. [T:SUS-107]

Dry the vegetables at 140 °F (60 °C) in a dehydrator or oven. [T:SUS-001]

Do not dry vegetables outdoors: dry them under the controlled conditions of an oven or dehydrator. [T:SUS-003]

Do not use trays or screens made of galvanized mesh; also avoid copper and aluminium mesh. [T:SUS-008]

Only use an oven if it can be set as low as 140 °F (60 °C); if it cannot go that low, the vegetables cook instead of drying. [T:SUS-069]

In an oven, keep 140–150 °F (about 60–66 °C): place a calibrated oven thermometer on the rack or tray and check it every 2 hours throughout drying. [T:SUS-070]

Leave the oven door open 2–3 inches (about 5–7.5 cm) so moisture can escape. [T:SUS-071]

In a conventional oven, drying can take up to twice as long as in a dehydrator, depending on air circulation. [T:SUS-072]

Dry the eggplant for 12–14 hours, until leathery to brittle. [T:SUS-116]

When drying is complete, some pieces are moister than others because of their size and location during drying; conditioning distributes residual moisture evenly and lowers the chance of spoilage, so condition these dried pieces. [T:SUS-203]

Put the cooled dried pieces in large plastic or glass containers about two-thirds full, cover lightly and keep them in a warm, dry, well-ventilated place for 4–10 days, stirring or shaking daily. [T:SUS-203]

If beads of moisture form inside, return the food to the dehydrator, then condition it again. [T:SUS-203]

## Packing and storing

Pack only completely cooled vegetables: warm dried food sweats, and that moisture can be enough for mould to grow. [T:SUS-024]

Store the dried vegetables in clean, dry canning jars, plastic freezer containers with tight-fitting lids or plastic freezer bags. [T:SUS-064]

Packaging must be food grade: do not use trash bags or plastic or fibreboard containers that previously held non-food items. [T:SUS-065]

Pack amounts that can be used at one time: each opening exposes the dried food to air and moisture, which lowers quality and can lead to spoilage. [T:SUS-067]

Vacuum packaging is not a preservation method by itself: removing oxygen increases the risk of botulism, and a product that needs refrigeration still needs it. [T:SUS-025]

Store the dried vegetables in a cool, dry, dark place. [T:SUS-066]

Dried vegetables keep about half as long as dried fruit: according to Clemson HGIC, about 6 months at 60 °F (about 15.6 °C) and 3 months at 80 °F (about 26.7 °C). [T:SUS-021]

According to MSU, if you soak dried vegetables, cover them with cold water until they nearly regain their original texture (1/2–2 hours); if you soak them longer than 2 hours, keep them in the refrigerator. [T:SUS-204]

## When to throw away

Check the dried vegetables often (glass shows condensation); if they have taken up moisture but are not spoiled, use them at once or dry them again and repack them. [T:SUS-023]

Throw away mouldy dried vegetables; also throw away any that smell off. [T:SUS-022]
