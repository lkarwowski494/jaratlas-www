# Smoked garlic and marjoram sausage (UGA)

This card describes a smoked and cooked garlic and marjoram sausage made with the UGA Extension recipe for Polish sausage (kielbasa). [T:MIE-139]

## Safety first

Too much nitrate or nitrite can be toxic: never add more than the recipe calls for. [T:MIE-011]

Use a ready-made premixed cure, diluted with salt, instead of weighing pure nitrite or nitrate, to lower the risk of a serious mistake. [T:MIE-012]

## Ingredients

Meat: 8 pounds pork shoulder or lean trim (75% lean) and 2 pounds beef trimmings (80% lean). [T:MIE-139]

Seasoning: salt 7 1/4 tablespoons (3 oz), sugar 4 tablespoons (1.6 oz), white pepper 2 1/2 tablespoons (0.5 oz), mustard seed 2 1/4 teaspoons (0.3 oz), marjoram 4 teaspoons (0.2 oz), garlic powder 0.1 oz and nutmeg 2 1/2 teaspoons (0.2 oz). [T:MIE-139]

Also: nonfat dry milk 1 3/8 cups (5.6 oz) and ice or water 4.8 oz. [T:MIE-139]

The recipe also calls for sodium nitrate and, optionally, sodium nitrite, in fractions of a teaspoon, and warns to use only the amounts given. [T:MIE-139]

## Curing salt

UGA strongly recommends a commercial premixed cure instead of pure nitrate or nitrite: use it in place of the amount of salt in the recipe, and it supplies the nitrite needed. [T:MIE-140]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): do not weigh pure nitrate or nitrite at home; use only a commercial premixed cure. [T:MIE-142]

In the EU, nitrite sold for food use may only be sold mixed with salt or a salt substitute, which is where curing salt comes from. [T:MIE-010]

In the EU, since 9 October 2025, heat-treated meat products that are not sterilised may contain at most 80 mg/kg of added nitrites. [T:MIE-008]

The EU also limits the nitrite left in the finished product from all sources to 45 mg/kg throughout its shelf life. [T:MIE-007]

## Making

Keep the meat and other ingredients refrigerated at 40 °F or below (about 4 °C), also while you work, and check with a thermometer. [T:MIE-138]

Grind the beef and pork through a 1/4-inch plate, add the spices and water, and mix thoroughly. [T:MIE-140]

Grind again through a 3/16-inch plate and stuff into natural hog casings. [T:MIE-140]

Hold the sausages overnight so the cure can react. [T:MIE-140]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): hold them in the refrigerator, because UGA says a product that will not be cooked or smoked right away goes back into the refrigerator as soon as it is made. [T:MIE-138]

## Smoking and cooking

Smoke at 90–100 °F for 2 hours, then raise the smokehouse temperature gradually to 165–170 °F. [T:MIE-031]

The UGA recipe stops at an internal temperature of 150 °F, but the same bulletin recommends 160 °F for products made from ground pork and beef. [T:MIE-031,MIE-021]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): cook until a thermometer shows 160 °F (about 71 °C) inside the sausage. [T:MIE-142]

A temperature of 144 °F is considered lethal to all trichinae. [T:MIE-026]

## Storing

According to UGA, cooked smoked sausages made with salt and nitrite keep 2–4 weeks in the refrigerator. [T:MIE-029]

## When to throw it away

Throw away smoked sausage that has been in the refrigerator for more than 4 weeks. [T:MIE-029]

Do not keep sausage out of the refrigerator for more than 2 hours, or for more than 1 hour when the temperature is above 90 °F (about 32 °C). [T:SLO-408]
