# Turkey breast jerky (PNW)

This card describes jerky made from thin strips of turkey breast, heated to 165 °F (about 74 °C) before drying and dried at 140 °F (60 °C). [T:SUS-342,MIE-038]

## !

- According to PNW 632, poultry jerky is done and safe only when the meat has been heated to 165 °F (about 74 °C) before or immediately after drying; check the strips with a thermometer, because a dehydrator alone does not ensure this. [T:SUS-342,MIE-105]

## Meat and preparation

According to PNW 632, the best poultry cuts for jerky are breast, thigh and leg; this card covers turkey breast. [T:SUS-342]

NCHFP generally does not recommend raw poultry for jerky because of the texture and flavour of the finished product. [T:MIE-042]

Raw meat can be contaminated with disease-causing microorganisms, which easily multiply on moist, high-protein foods. [T:MIE-100]

Keep the meat refrigerated at 40 °F (about 4 °C) or below, and use raw poultry within 2 days or freeze it. [T:MIE-110]

Remove the skin and fat before drying. [T:SUS-342]

Cut the meat into strips no thicker than 1/4 inch (about 6 mm). [T:MIE-040]

This card does not cover jerky made from ground meat: disease-causing microorganisms are more difficult to eliminate in it than in whole-muscle strips. [T:MIE-115]

Marinate the meat in the refrigerator; do not save and re-use the marinade. [T:MIE-109]

## Heating before drying

Heat the turkey strips to 165 °F (about 74 °C) before drying. [T:SUS-342]

A dehydrator alone does not kill microorganisms, it only inactivates them; most dehydrator instructions lack the heating step. [T:MIE-105]

One way is to bring the strips and marinade to a boil and boil for 5 minutes, then drain the strips. [T:MIE-037]

Another way, according to Penn State, is to bake the strips on racks in a 325 °F (163 °C) oven. [T:MIE-108]

Either way, check several strips with a metal stem-type thermometer; strips thicker than 1/4 inch may need a longer boil in the marinade. [T:MIE-107]

If any strip has not reached 165 °F (about 74 °C), keep heating and measure again; do not dry strips that have not reached this temperature. [T:SUS-342,MIE-107]

JarAtlas rule (cautious): this card heats turkey only before drying, when each strip can be checked with a thermometer; it gives no oven time for heating after drying. [T:SUS-342]

## Drying

Dry the strips in a dehydrator or oven preheated to 140 °F (60 °C). [T:MIE-038]

Do not dry meat outdoors: dry it under the controlled conditions of an oven or dehydrator. [T:SUS-003]

Do not use trays or screens made of galvanized mesh; also avoid copper and aluminium mesh. [T:SUS-008]

Dry the jerky until a test piece cracks but does not break when bent; strips heated in marinade dry faster than unheated ones, so begin checking them after 3 hours. [T:MIE-112]

## Packing and storing

After drying, pat off beads of oil with absorbent towels, cool the jerky and package it in glass jars or heavy plastic food storage bags. [T:MIE-113]

Packaging must be food grade: do not use trash bags or plastic or fibreboard containers that previously held non-food items. [T:SUS-065]

NCHFP calls vacuum packaging a good option, but it is not a preservation method by itself: removing oxygen increases the risk of botulism, and a product that needs refrigeration still needs it. [T:MIE-113,SUS-025]

Keep properly dried jerky in a sealed container at room temperature for no more than two weeks (NCHFP); longer in the refrigerator or freezer. [T:MIE-041]

## When to throw away

Check the dried food often (glass shows condensation); if it has taken up moisture but is not spoiled, use it at once or dry it again and repack it. [T:SUS-023]

Throw away mouldy jerky; also throw away any that smells off. [T:SUS-022]
